## Defect
1. Period in Request 46. For Request for Production No. 46 the ideal answer gives the period for corporate organizational charts as "2001-2011". The request in the task's source document asks for charts "for the period 2014 to the present". The period in the ideal answer appears nowhere in the task documents as a period and predates both the defined Relevant Time Period (from January 1, 2014) and the vehicles in issue (model year 2016 to present).

2. Two headings. The text of Request 5 is headed "REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION NO. 6", so the answer carries two headings for Request No. 6 and none for Request No. 5; the response that follows is correctly headed "RESPONSE TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION NO. 5". The response to Request 33 is headed "RESPONSE TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION NO. 34", so the answer carries two responses headed No. 34 and none headed No. 33. In each case the request text under the heading is the request served under the correct number (Request 5 on page 6, Request 33 on page 9 of the source document). In a document whose responses are cross-referenced by number ("*See* Firebolt's Responses and Objections to Request Nos. 1, 7, 30"), a misnumbered heading misidentifies the request to which an objection is addressed.

3. Superseded scope standard. The phrase "not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence" appears 37 times: in General Objections 7 and 8 (to the defined terms "Rims" and "Relevant Time Period") and in the specific objections to 35 requests. The action is in the United States District Court for the District of New Jersey, so the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure govern discovery, and the task prompt asks for responses "in accordance with Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 34". The December 1, 2015 amendments to Rule 26(b)(1) deleted the sentence that contained the "reasonably calculated" phrase and defined the scope of discovery as nonprivileged matter "relevant to any party's claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case". The task is set in 2026 (the meeting notes are dated January 30, 2026). An objection framed on the deleted phrase invokes a test that the current rule does not contain, and the word "proportional" does not appear anywhere in the ideal answer although essential criterion 7 asks for objections on "disproportionality".

4. Subject-matter scope. General Objection 11 commits Firebolt to provide trade secrets and other confidential information "[t]o the extent that such information is relevant to the subject matter of this litigation". Before December 1, 2015, Rule 26(b)(1) allowed the court, for good cause, to "order discovery of any matter relevant to the subject matter involved in the action"; the 2015 amendments deleted that sentence together with the "reasonably calculated" sentence, and the current rule states no scope measured by the subject matter of the action. The commitment is framed on a standard the current rule does not contain and that is wider than relevance to a claim or defense, in a document whose General Objection 4 objects to every request "to the extent it seeks information beyond the scope of discovery contemplated by Rule 26(b)".

5. Discovery device. The paragraph introducing the General Objections states that they "apply to each separate interrogatory ("Request")". The document responds to requests for production under Rule 34, as its title and introduction state; interrogatories are a separate device governed by Rule 33, and every request served in the task is a request for production. The sentence that incorporates the general objections into each specific response therefore names a device the document does not answer.

6. Rubric criterion 11. Essential criterion 11 (line 30) requires objections to the key defined terms and objections "that the requests using them are unduly burdensome or not likely to lead to admissible evidence." The second ground restates the deleted "reasonably calculated" test. A response that objects to the requests using the defined terms only on that ground meets the criterion, although the ground is not in the current rule; a response that objects to them only for lack of relevance or proportionality, the two limits the current rule does state, meets it only by arguing burden.

7. Points the essential criteria require. The README says the editors "author the rubric criteria from that answer" and that essential elements are "scored against the ideal response", but the ideal answer omits four points that essential criteria call for and that the meeting notes instruct: it never refuses to produce a customer list (criterion 4: "refusal to produce customer lists"; notes: "We are NOT providing any sort of customer list."); it does not object that the putative class period has no end (criterion 11: "including the lack of an end-date for the putative period"; notes: "Objection: there is no end to the putative class period – and lack thereof makes some requests impossible to answer"); it does not say that dealers and their service centers are independent (criterion 12: "note independent dealer/service center status to explain limits on responsive records"; notes: "Firebolt dealers and their service centers are independent entities" and "Firebolt can only request records from the dealers"); and the response to Request 9, which asks for "the identities of all purchasers and lessees", states objections without declining production (criterion 22: "decline to produce customer-identifying data"). General Objection 8 objects to the Relevant Time Period only because "it seeks information beyond the scope of the alleged class period"; the complaint (page 2) defines the class as purchasers or lessees "from 2016 to present". The word "independent" occurs in the ideal answer only twice, in Request 29 and in its response ("independent contractors"); "customer list", "end date" and "end-date" occur 0 times.

8. Answer type and typographical errors. `ideal_answer_type` (line 53) is "Email". The README defines the field as "The expected output form (e.g. Memo, Letter, Email)"; the ideal answer is a captioned discovery response with no email heading or salutation, and task 2-005, whose answer is not a memo, letter or email, uses "Other". The ideal answer also carries 24 character-recognition and typing errors: "tem1s", "infom1ation", "pem1issible", "va6rue", "info1mation", "desi6rn"; stray periods in "issue.in", "seeking. information", "to. the extent", "by the. attorney-client", "business and. trade" and "general and. specific"; "Firebolt' s"; two single quotation marks in place of a double one in four quoted terms (''Vehicles", "Plaintiffs,'', ''tested,", ''agents,"); a stray "·" before "production"; semicolons for commas in "realized or potential;" and "repair, replace; equip"; a missing closing quotation mark after "Vehicles" in the response to Request 35; seven missing commas, counted here as one error, in the quoted terms of the response to Request 29 ("potential " "problems " ... "recall "); a missing full stop at the end of the first paragraph of the response to Request 10; and "responds and objects as follows to Plaintiffs' First Request [...] as follows:" in the introduction.

9. Rule 34(b)(2)(B) and (C) and Local Civil Rule 34.1. None of the objections in the ideal answer states whether any responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of that objection ("withh" occurs 0 times), which Rule 34(b)(2)(C) has required since December 1, 2015: "An objection must state whether any responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of that objection." The response to Request 30 agrees to produce documents only "after the parties have had an opportunity to further meet and confer and reach an agreement regarding the proper scope of this Request and the proper search terms and parameters" and specifies no time; under Rule 34(b)(2)(B), amended on the same date, the production "must then be completed no later than the time for inspection specified in the request or another reasonable time specified in the response", and the requests specify production "within thirty (30) days of service" (page 1). Local Civil Rule 34.1, which the prompt invokes, provides: "When any privilege is claimed, the party asserting it shall indicate, as to the information requested, whether any such documents exist."; the privilege objections in the ideal answer (for example, "Firebolt also objects to the extent this Request seeks confidential information or information covered by the attorney-client privilege, the work product doctrine, business and trade secret privileges, or any other privileges or immunities from discovery authorized by law.", which occurs 11 times) do not state whether any such documents exist. No rubric criterion tests any of these points (essential criterion 2 asks only that the introduction cite Local Civil Rule 34.1), and the task documents do not state what Firebolt withholds on any objection, whether any privileged documents exist, or when its search for the Request 30 documents will be complete, so no replacement text is proposed for these statements: a correction would have to supply facts the record does not contain.

## Objections considered
- Objection: No effect: nothing is graded on the restated period or on the heading numbers.
  Answer: Agreed that no criterion turns on them, and the severity is low; the README says model output is compared with the ideal answer point by point, and a misnumbered heading attaches an objection to the wrong request in a document whose responses cross-refer by number.
- Objection: Defense counsel still use "not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence" in practice, and the phrase is not sanctionable in itself.
  Answer: The phrase no longer appears in Rule 26(b)(1); the 2015 amendments deleted the sentence that contained it and stated the scope of discovery as relevance to a claim or defense and proportionality. A reference answer drafted in 2026 for a federal action, under a prompt that invokes the Federal Rules, should state the objection in the terms of the current rule; the replacement keeps every objection and changes only the ground stated.
- Objection: The replacement adds a proportionality assertion the original did not make.
  Answer: The replacement is disjunctive ("not relevant [...] or not proportional", and in the specific objections "unrelated to the claims or defenses [...] or not proportional"), which is the negation of the rule's conjunctive scope and so asserts only that the request falls outside the scope of discovery. Each changed objection already calls the request "overbroad" and "unduly burdensome", which are the burden and breadth grounds that the proportionality limit covers.
- Objection: "Interrogatory" is a slip that misleads no reader.
  Answer: It sits in the sentence that incorporates the general objections into every specific response, which essential criterion 3 grades; as written it attaches them to a device the document does not answer. The change replaces one word and alters no objection.
- Objection: A producing party may agree to produce more than the rule requires, so General Objection 11 misstates nothing.
  Answer: A party may agree to broader production, but the reference answer frames the commitment on the subject-matter standard deleted in 2015 while objecting in General Objection 4 to every request beyond the scope of Rule 26(b). The replacement states the commitment in the terms of the current rule and still meets essential criterion 5, which asks that relevant confidential information be produced, if at all, under the protective order.
- Objection: Changing criterion 11 changes scoring.
  Answer: A response that objects to the requests using the defined terms as unduly burdensome meets the criterion before and after the change. A response that objects to them only as "not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence" no longer meets it, because that ground has not been in Rule 26(b)(1) since December 1, 2015. A response that objects to them only for lack of relevance to a claim or defense or for disproportionality, the grounds the current rule states, meets it after the change without having to argue burden.
- Objection: Adding the customer-list refusal, the end-date objection and the independent-dealer sentence rewrites the attorney's work product.
  Answer: Each addition is the meeting notes' own instruction in the notes' words, each is required by an essential criterion that the README says was authored from the ideal answer, and without them the reference answer fails four of its own essential criteria. No rubric text changes for these points.
- Objection: The ideal answer also omits the statement that Rule 34(b)(2)(C) has required since December 1, 2015, whether any responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of each objection, and gives no time for the production it agrees to make; those points should be corrected in the same change.
  Answer: Whether documents are withheld on a given objection, and when they will be produced, are facts about Firebolt's collection that the task documents do not supply, so a correction would have to supply facts the record does not contain. The omissions are therefore recorded rather than corrected: a response that adds the statements meets every criterion, and no criterion credits a response for leaving them out.
- Objection: "Other" is no more informative than "Email".
  Answer: The README defines the field as the expected output form; the expected output here is a captioned discovery response, which is not an email, and the sample uses "Other" for task 2-005, whose answer is not a memo, letter or email.
