## Defect
The federal practice reference for the document-discovery skill (skills/litigation/document-discovery/references/federal-practice.md, and the same lines of the generated copy at plugins/legalquants-litigation/skills/document-discovery/references/federal-practice.md) summarises the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and Evidence that govern discovery. The correction belongs in skills/ because plugins/ is generated by the pack command. The Rule 45 summary (lines 92, 94 and 96) misstates the Rule in seven places, and the summaries of Rules 26, 33, 34, 36 and 37 and FRE 501 and 502 (lines 36 to 102) misstate those rules in seventeen further paragraphs. The skill's drafting-patterns reference (skills/litigation/document-discovery/references/drafting-patterns.md, and its generated copy under plugins/legalquants-litigation/) misstates Rule 33 in one paragraph, Rule 36(a)(4) in another and Rule 26(b)(5)(B) in a third (items 31, 32 and 33).

Rule 45

1. Line 94, after correctly stating that the compliance court must quash or modify a subpoena on the four mandatory grounds, adds: "It may protect a person from significant expense." Rule 45(d)(2)(B)(ii) is mandatory, not permissive, and it is narrower in two respects: it applies when production or inspection is ordered after a written objection, and it protects a person who is neither a party nor a party's officer. A user triaging a nonparty subpoena from this reference would treat cost protection as a matter of discretion and would not be told who is entitled to it or when.
2. Line 94 ends the four mandatory grounds "with the rule's specified alternatives". Rule 45(d)(3)(C) permits the court, "in the circumstances described in Rule 45(d)(3)(B)", to order appearance or production under specified conditions instead of quashing or modifying; it does not reach the (d)(3)(A) grounds, for which the court has no alternative to quashing or modifying. The sentence can be read as telling the user that a court faced with a privileged-matter or undue-burden subpoena may instead impose conditions.
3. Line 92 says "The issuing court is generally the court where the action is pending; compliance is usually within the rule's geographic limits". Rule 45(a)(2) admits no exception: "A subpoena must issue from the court where the action is pending." Rule 45(c) states the places at which a subpoena may command compliance, and Rule 45(d)(3)(A)(ii) requires the compliance court, on timely motion, to quash or modify a subpoena that requires a person to comply beyond those limits; "usually" presents them as a description of practice.
4. The first sentence of line 92 says that service is permitted nationwide "subject to the rule's ... geographic limits". Rule 45(b)(2) permits service "at any place within the United States", and the geographical limits of Rule 45(c) govern the place of compliance, not the place of service.
5. Line 92 says "A party must take reasonable steps to avoid imposing undue burden or expense on a person subject to the subpoena." Rule 45(d)(1) places that duty on "A party or attorney responsible for issuing and serving a subpoena" and requires the compliance court to enforce it and impose an appropriate sanction on a party or attorney who fails to comply.
6. Line 96 says "Rule 45 incorporates a privilege-log and clawback track through Rule 26(b)(5)." Rule 45 does not refer to Rule 26(b)(5). Rule 45(e)(2) supplies its own track for a person withholding or producing subpoenaed information, in terms parallel to Rule 26(b)(5), and Rule 45(e)(2)(B) directs the presentation of produced information for a determination of the claim to the court for the district where compliance is required, where Rule 26(b)(5)(B) says "the court".
7. Line 92 says "Before serving a subpoena commanding production, ESI, tangible things, or premises inspection, the serving party must serve each party with notice and a copy as Rule 45(a)(4) provides." Rule 45(a)(4) requires the notice and copy only "If the subpoena commands the production of documents, electronically stored information, or tangible things or the inspection of premises before trial". The sentence drops "before trial" and so states the duty for a trial subpoena as well.

Rules 26, 33, 34, 36 and 37, and FRE 502

8. Line 36 lists among the Rule 26(b)(1) proportionality factors "the parties' relative access to information". The Rule's factor is "the parties’ relative access to relevant information"; access to information in general is not what the Rule weighs.
9. Line 38 paraphrases the Rule 26(b)(2)(C) limitations as cases where "the request ... gives the requesting party ample opportunity" and where the material "can be obtained from a more convenient or less burdensome source". Rule 26(b)(2)(C)(ii) concerns a party seeking discovery that "has had ample opportunity to obtain the information by discovery in the action"; the request gives no one an opportunity. Rule 26(b)(2)(C)(i) also reaches a source that is "less expensive", which the paraphrase omits.
10. Line 38 says a court "may order discovery for good cause subject to Rule 26(b)(1) limits" from inaccessible electronically stored information. Rule 26(b)(2)(B) conditions that order on good cause shown by the requesting party, "considering the limitations of Rule 26(b)(2)(C)", not Rule 26(b)(1), places on the responding party the burden of showing that the information is not reasonably accessible, and allows the court to specify conditions.
11. Line 40 says "Rule 26(b)(3) protects trial-preparation materials prepared by or for a party or representative" and that ordinary materials "require substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship", while "an attorney's mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, or legal theories receive heightened protection". Rule 26(b)(3)(A) reaches documents and tangible things prepared in anticipation of litigation or for trial by or for another party or its representative, and allows their discovery, subject to Rule 26(b)(4), if they are otherwise discoverable under Rule 26(b)(1) and the party shows substantial need for them to prepare its case and that it cannot, without undue hardship, obtain their substantial equivalent by other means; the sentence drops the first of the two conditions. Rule 26(b)(3)(B) requires a court that orders discovery of those materials to protect against disclosure of the mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, or legal theories of "a party’s attorney or other representative concerning the litigation", which is wider than the attorney's and is a duty of the court.
12. Line 44 limits Rule 26(b)(5)(B) to "a notice that information was inadvertently produced", omits the receiving party's duty that it "must not use or disclose the information until the claim is resolved", and says the receiving party, "if disputed", presents the information to the court. The Rule applies to information produced in discovery that is subject to a claim of privilege or of trial-preparation protection, whether or not the claim rests on inadvertence; it imposes the bar on use and disclosure; and presentation to the court under seal is permissive ("may promptly present"), not a required step and not conditioned on a dispute.
13. Line 52 says the parties must hold the Rule 26(f) conference "unless the court orders otherwise". Rule 26(f)(1) also excepts "a proceeding exempted from initial disclosure under Rule 26(a)(1)(B)", which the sentence omits, so the sentence states the duty for every case.
14. The next sentence of line 52 gives the conference subjects as "claims and defenses, initial disclosures, preservation, and a discovery plan." Rule 26(f)(2) also requires the parties to consider "the possibilities for promptly settling or resolving the case", which the list omits, and states each subject as a duty of the parties.
15. Line 56 says the Rule 26(g) certification covers a request, response or objection that "is not unreasonable, unduly burdensome, or unduly expensive in light of the case needs". Rule 26(g)(1)(B)(iii) measures that against "the needs of the case, prior discovery in the case, the amount in controversy, and the importance of the issues at stake in the action"; the sentence drops three of the four factors.
16. Line 56 says "An unsigned paper can be stricken, and sanctions can follow without substantial justification." Rule 26(g)(2) says the court "must strike it unless a signature is promptly supplied after the omission is called to the attorney’s or party’s attention", and Rule 26(g)(3) says that if a certification violates the rule without substantial justification the court "must impose an appropriate sanction". Both are mandatory; the sentence states both as discretionary.
17. Line 60 says Rule 33 "generally limits a party to 25 interrogatories", that interrogatories may seek "opinions or contentions" without qualification, and that "Answers and objections are written, separately and fully, under oath". Rule 33(a)(1) limits the interrogatories a party may serve "on any other party"; Rule 33(a)(2) protects only an interrogatory that asks for "an opinion or contention that relates to fact or the application of law to fact"; and Rule 33(b)(3) requires answers, not objections, to be given under oath, while Rule 33(b)(5) requires the attorney who objects to sign the objections.
18. Line 62 says "an unstated ground is ordinarily waived unless the court excuses the failure for good cause." Rule 33(b)(4) says "Any ground not stated in a timely objection is waived unless the court, for good cause, excuses the failure"; "ordinarily" presents a rule without exception other than the court's excuse as a tendency, and the sentence drops "timely". The same line says a party answering under Rule 33(d) "must identify the records with enough detail to locate them"; Rule 33(d)(1) requires detail sufficient "to enable the interrogating party to locate and identify them as readily as the responding party could", and the same line's "provide a reasonable opportunity to examine, audit, or copy them" turns the conjunctive requirement of Rule 33(d)(2), a reasonable opportunity "to examine and audit the records and to make copies, compilations, abstracts, or summaries", into a choice of any one of them. The line also calls Local Civil Rule 33.3 "SDNY/EDNY Rule 33.3"; the joint local rules title it "Interrogatories (Southern District Only)".
19. Line 66 says a Rule 34 request reaches material in "the responding person's possession, custody, or control" and that each item or category "must be described with reasonable particularity and may specify a reasonable time, place, or manner." Rule 34(a) speaks of "the responding party’s possession, custody, or control" (a nonparty is reached under Rule 34(c) and Rule 45), and Rule 34(b)(1)(B) says the request "must specify a reasonable time, place, and manner for the inspection and for performing the related acts". Only the form for producing electronically stored information is optional (Rule 34(b)(1)(C)).
20. Line 68 says the response states "the grounds for objection", that "An objection must state whether responsive materials are being withheld", and that "ESI is produced in the form ordinarily maintained or another reasonably usable form". Rule 34(b)(2)(B) requires the response to "state with specificity the grounds for objecting to the request, including the reasons"; Rule 34(b)(2)(C) requires a statement whether materials are being withheld "on the basis of that objection"; and Rule 34(b)(2)(E)(ii) supplies the default forms only "If a request does not specify a form for producing electronically stored information", and Rule 34(b)(2)(E) applies "Unless otherwise stipulated or ordered by the court". The sentence states the default forms without the condition that no form was specified.
21. Line 70 quotes the phrase removed from Rule 26(b)(1) in 2015 as "“reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence”". The former text, as shown in the Federal Judicial Center's 2015 redline that the file itself cites (FJC-REDLINE), was "reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence".
22. Line 74 says a request for admission "may concern facts, application of law to fact, or genuineness of documents" and that "An answer must admit, deny, or explain why the matter cannot truthfully be admitted or denied after reasonable inquiry." Rule 36(a)(1)(A) also reaches "opinions about either", and Rule 36(a)(4) requires an answer that does not admit to "specifically deny it or state in detail why the answering party cannot truthfully admit or deny it", and allows lack of knowledge or information as a reason only if the party states that it has made reasonable inquiry and that the information it knows or can readily obtain is insufficient.
23. Line 76 says "Rule 37(c)(2) can shift reasonable expenses when a party fails to admit a matter later proven." Under Rule 37(c)(2), on the requesting party's motion, "The court must so order unless" one of four listed exceptions applies. The award is mandatory subject to the exceptions.
24. Line 80 says "Rule 37(a)(2) assigns venue for motions involving a party and a nonparty deponent, while a documents-only nonparty subpoena ordinarily follows Rule 45's compliance-court path." Rule 37(a)(2) is not confined to deponents: a motion for an order to a party must be made in the court where the action is pending, and a motion for an order to a nonparty in the court where the discovery is or will be taken. Where a person commanded to produce documents or tangible things or to permit inspection serves a written objection, Rule 45(d)(2)(B)(i) gives the serving party a motion in the court for the district where compliance is required; "ordinarily" presents that rule as a tendency.
25. Line 86 says that under Rule 37(e)(2) "a finding that the party acted with intent to deprive another party of the information permits a presumption or adverse-inference instruction and potentially dismissal or default." Rule 37(e)(2) allows those measures "only upon finding that the party acted with the intent to deprive another party of the information’s use in the litigation". The sentence drops "only" and the object of the intent the Rule requires, the information's use in the litigation.
26. Line 100 says "FRE 502(a) can produce subject-matter waiver after an intentional disclosure in a federal proceeding when fairness requires it" and "Rule 502(e) makes a party agreement binding on the parties unless incorporated into a court order." FRE 502(a) applies to a disclosure made "in a federal proceeding or to a federal office or agency" and extends a waiver to undisclosed communications or information "in a federal or state proceeding" "only if" three conditions are all met: the waiver is intentional, the disclosed and undisclosed communications or information concern the same subject matter, and they ought in fairness to be considered together. FRE 502(e) makes an agreement on the effect of disclosure in a federal proceeding "binding only on the parties to the agreement, unless it is incorporated into a court order". Without "only", the sentence does not say what the Rule's point is, that an agreement binds only its parties and that incorporation into an order is what extends its effect beyond them. The same line states FRE 502(b) without its setting or its effect: the Rule applies to a disclosure "made in a federal proceeding or to a federal office or agency" and provides that it "does not operate as a waiver in a federal or state proceeding" if the disclosure is inadvertent, "the holder of the privilege or protection took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure" and the holder promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error. It says FRE 502(c) "addresses state proceedings"; Rule 502(c) governs whether a disclosure made in a state proceeding, and not the subject of a state-court order concerning waiver, operates as a waiver in a federal proceeding, and does not govern its effect in the state proceeding. And it describes a Rule 502(d) order as one "that disclosure does not waive privilege" without the Rule's limit to "disclosure connected with the litigation pending before the court".
27. Line 52 says "The plan addresses timing, subjects, phasing, limitations, ESI preservation and production forms, privilege claims and log timing or method, and whether to seek a Rule 502 order." Rule 26(f)(3) requires the discovery plan to state the parties' views and proposals on six subjects. The sentence omits (F) entirely; omits from (A) the form or requirement of the Rule 26(a) disclosures and the statement of when initial disclosures were made or will be made, its one word "timing", listed with subjects and phasing, reading as the (B) question of when discovery should be completed; reduces electronically stored information to its preservation and production forms, where (C) reaches "any issues about disclosure, discovery, or preservation"; and states the FRE 502 order without the condition of (D), that the parties "agree on a procedure to assert these claims after production".
28. Line 74 says "a qualified answer must specify the part admitted or denied." Rule 36(a)(4) provides that "when good faith requires that a party qualify an answer or deny only a part of a matter, the answer must specify the part admitted and qualify or deny the rest"; the sentence omits the good-faith condition and the duty to qualify or deny the rest.
29. Line 80 says "A motion to compel must include the certification that the movant conferred or attempted to confer in good faith to obtain the disclosure without court action." Rule 37(a)(1) requires a certification that the movant has in good faith conferred or attempted to confer "with the person or party failing to make disclosure or discovery in an effort to obtain it without court action"; the sentence confines the conferral to disclosure, leaving out discovery, the more common subject of a motion to compel, and omits the person or party with whom the movant must confer.
30. Line 102 says "circuit law controls privilege elements, litigation-purpose questions, common-interest or confidentiality issues, and waiver details." FRE 501 provides that "in a civil case, state law governs privilege regarding a claim or defense for which state law supplies the rule of decision"; for such a claim or defense, state law, not circuit law, supplies the elements of the privilege. The file's own FRE-2025 ledger row and line 100 recognize that rule; line 102 states circuit law without the exception. Where state law governs privilege, FRE 502 still governs waiver by disclosure in the circumstances it sets out: under FRE 502(f), "notwithstanding Rule 501, this rule applies even if state law provides the rule of decision."

Drafting patterns

31. drafting-patterns.md line 74 says "A response may answer, object specifically, or state that the investigation is continuing with a concrete supplementation plan." Rule 33(b)(3) provides that "Each interrogatory must, to the extent it is not objected to, be answered separately and fully in writing under oath"; a statement that the investigation is continuing is not an answer, and the time to answer may be extended only by stipulation under Rule 29 or by court order (Rule 33(b)(2), "A shorter or longer time may be stipulated to under Rule 29 or be ordered by the court"; see also Rule 6(b)(1)), or the answer deferred for a contention interrogatory by an order under Rule 33(a)(2). The same line repeats the Rule 33(d) wording of item 18: a records-based response "must identify the records in enough detail to locate them and provide a reasonable opportunity to examine, audit, or copy them".
32. drafting-patterns.md line 84 says "The response should admit, deny, or explain after reasonable inquiry why it cannot truthfully admit or deny, and should fairly address any part that can be admitted." Rule 36(a)(4) requires an answer that does not admit to "specifically deny it or state in detail why the answering party cannot truthfully admit or deny it"; it attaches reasonable inquiry to one reason only, lack of knowledge or information, which may be asserted "only if the party states that it has made reasonable inquiry and that the information it knows or can readily obtain is insufficient to enable it to admit or deny"; it requires a denial to "fairly respond to the substance of the matter"; and "when good faith requires that a party qualify an answer or deny only a part of a matter, the answer must specify the part admitted and qualify or deny the rest". The line drops "specifically" and "in detail", attaches reasonable inquiry to every inability, and replaces the good-faith qualification rule with "should fairly address any part that can be admitted", so a response that explains an inability without detail, or admits a part without qualifying or denying the rest, follows the pattern.
33. drafting-patterns.md line 180 says "For an inadvertent-production notice, record the notice and hold the item while counsel applies Rule 26(b)(5)(B), FRE 502, and any Rule 502(d) order". This repeats the framing of item 12. Rule 26(b)(5)(B) applies to a notice that information produced in discovery is subject to a claim of privilege or of protection as trial-preparation material, whether or not the claim rests on inadvertence, and the bar on use and disclosure attaches on notice. A notice of a claim over information that is not said to have been produced inadvertently falls outside the instruction, and the item is not held.

## Objections considered
- Objection: The "may protect" sentence is the only error; the preceding sentence is a fair compression of 45(d)(3).
  Answer: The sentence is ambiguous rather than false; the replacement is offered because "with the rule's specified alternatives" can be read as (d)(3)(C), which is limited by its own words to the (d)(3)(B) circumstances. The replacement separates the mandatory grounds, the permissive grounds and the conditions alternative, and keeps the Rule's qualifiers ("if no exception or waiver applies"; "or other confidential research, development, or commercial information"; the full (d)(3)(B)(ii) description of an unretained expert's opinion or information; "cannot be otherwise met without undue hardship").
- Objection: The sentence paraphrases the court's discretion under Rule 45(d)(3)(C) to order production on conditions including reasonable compensation, so 'may' is right.
  Answer: 'Significant expense' is the phrase of (d)(2)(B)(ii) and appears nowhere else in the Rules; (d)(3)(C) is confined to the (d)(3)(B) grounds and speaks of the person being 'reasonably compensated'. The sentence follows the (d)(3)(A) sentence without any reference to trade secrets or experts.
- Objection: The proposed sentence is itself incomplete or wrong: it says 'written objection' and 'compelled', which the rule does not say in terms.
  Answer: Rule 45(d)(2)(B) provides for 'a written objection' and (i) for 'an order compelling production or inspection'; (ii) attaches the protection to that order. The proposed sentence uses the rule's own words for the protected person and cites the paragraph.
- Objection: "Generally" and "usually" describe practice, not the rule, and a reader will check the rule.
  Answer: Rule 45(a)(2) says a subpoena "must issue from the court where the action is pending" and admits no exception; a subpoena that commands compliance beyond the Rule 45(c) limits must be quashed or modified on timely motion under Rule 45(d)(3)(A)(ii). The replacement keeps the file's point that the compliance court may differ from the issuing court.
- Objection: "A party" is shorthand for the serving party, and the sanction sentence adds detail a triage summary does not need.
  Answer: The duty under Rule 45(d)(1) rests on the party or attorney responsible for issuing and serving the subpoena, not on a party who did not issue or serve it, and it binds the issuing attorney, whom the file's sentence omits. The enforcement sentence is mandatory ("must enforce this duty and impose an appropriate sanction") and is what makes the duty actionable for the person subject to the subpoena; the replacement states it in the Rule's words with the dashes replaced by commas.
- Objection: Rule 26(b)(5) governs privilege claims in all discovery, including subpoenas, so "incorporates" is harmless.
  Answer: Rule 26(b)(5)(A) speaks of "a party" withholding information; Rule 45(e)(2)(A) speaks of "A person withholding subpoenaed information", which reaches a nonparty, and Rule 45(e)(2)(B) directs the presentation for a determination of the claim to the court for the district where compliance is required. Rule 45 contains no cross-reference to Rule 26(b)(5).
- Objection: The file is a research aid that says the rule text controls, so severity is overstated and the fix is unnecessary.
  Answer: The disclaimer does not make a permissive statement of a mandatory rule correct; line 94 uses 'must' for (d)(3)(A), so the file distinguishes the two. The same pattern recurs at line 56 (Rule 26(g)(2) and (3)), line 66 (Rule 34(b)(1)(B)) and line 76 (Rule 37(c)(2)), each of which the user would read as discretion.
- Objection: Rule 26(b)(5)(B) is in practice invoked after inadvertent production, so "inadvertently produced" is a fair description.
  Answer: The Rule's trigger is a claim of privilege or protection over produced information; whether the production waived the privilege, including whether it was inadvertent under FRE 502(b), is the question the claim procedure lets the court decide. Describing the trigger as inadvertence would lead a user to resist a clawback notice because the production was deliberate, while the Rule obliges the receiving party to sequester and not to use or disclose the information until the claim is resolved. The replacement uses the Rule's trigger and lists the four duties and the option in the Rule's order.
- Objection: "Ordinarily" in the Rule 33(b)(4) sentence, and line 110's caution against saying an objection is "automatically waived", show that the file means the court's power to excuse.
  Answer: The Rule's only exception is the court's excuse for good cause, which the sentence states separately; "ordinarily" adds a second, unstated exception. The replacement keeps the excuse and adds the Rule's "timely".
- Objection: The Rule 36, Rule 33(a)(2), Rule 26(b)(1), Rule 26(b)(3), Rule 26(f)(2), Rule 33(d)(2) and FRE 502(a) to (d) changes complete paraphrases that were not wrong.
  Answer: Each paraphrase states a legal list or condition and leaves out an element that changes the result: a request to admit an opinion about facts or their application is proper under Rule 36(a)(1)(A) and the file's list excludes it; an interrogatory seeking a contention on a pure question of law is not protected by Rule 33(a)(2), and the file's unqualified "opinions or contentions" includes it; the proportionality factor is access to relevant information, not to information generally; trial-preparation materials that are not otherwise discoverable under Rule 26(b)(1) cannot be reached by a showing of need and hardship; settlement is a subject the parties must consider at the Rule 26(f) conference; a party answering under Rule 33(d) must allow examination and audit and the making of copies, compilations, abstracts or summaries, not any one of them; a disclosure to a federal office or agency is within FRE 502(a) and (b), the three conditions of each are cumulative, and both state their effect "in a federal or state proceeding", which is what lets a federal ruling bind a state court; Rule 502(c) does not govern the effect of a disclosure in the state proceeding where it was made; and a Rule 502(d) order reaches only disclosure connected with the litigation pending before the court. The replacements follow the text clause by clause.
- Objection: Line 88 already says that negligence is not enough for the severe measures, so the missing "only" in the Rule 37(e)(2) sentence does no harm.
  Answer: Line 88 excludes negligence; the Rule excludes every state of mind short of the intent to deprive another party of the information's use in the litigation, and makes that finding the sole gateway to the three measures. The sentence as written says such a finding "permits" the measures, which leaves open other routes to them, and it omits the object of the intent.
- Objection: Rule 37(a)(2)'s nonparty clause is used mainly for deponents, and a document subpoena is enforced under Rule 45, so the sentence describes practice correctly.
  Answer: The Rule's words reach a motion for an order to any nonparty, and the Rule 45(d)(2)(B)(i) motion is the route the Rule provides once a written objection is served, not what ordinarily happens. The replacement states both rules in their own words and keeps the file's point that a document subpoena is enforced in the court for the district where compliance is required.
- Objection: The misquotation of the former Rule 26(b)(1) phrase is immaterial because the phrase is no longer law.
  Answer: The file presents the words in quotation marks as the phrase the 2015 amendments removed; a user who quotes or searches for it will not find it in the former rule. The correction restores "the discovery of", as the Federal Judicial Center redline the file cites shows it.
- Objection: Line 84 of drafting-patterns.md is drafting advice ("should"), not a statement of Rule 36.
