## Defect
The source ledger in the federal practice reference lists EEOC v. BDO USA, LLP as authority on privilege logs, links the file 16-20314-CV0.pdf, dates it "Revised May 8, 2017" and rates it "Binding only in Fifth Circuit; high for holding, medium outside". That file is the original panel opinion (856 F.3d 356). On November 16, 2017 the same panel withdrew it and substituted a new opinion, published at 16-20314-CV1.pdf. The linked file carries no withdrawal notice. The general propositions in the row survive in the substituted opinion as statements of the standard: a log must give enough information to test the claim, a bare "legal" label is conclusory, and the proponent bears the burden. What does not survive is the withdrawn opinion's application of them. The withdrawn opinion held that some of BDO's log entries were too vague or incomplete and that BDO had not proved its prima facie case; the substituted opinion states that the court "will not analyze the privilege logs in the first instance" and remands. A lawyer relying on the row could cite a withdrawn opinion, and could cite it for a ruling on the sufficiency of a log that the operative opinion does not make. The row appears at line 20 of skills/litigation/document-discovery/references/federal-practice.md and in the generated copy under plugins/legalquants-litigation/.

Five further rows of the same ledger (lines 21 to 25) misdescribe their sources:

1. Line 21, SDNY-EDNY, lists "Rule 33.3 interrogatory sequencing" among the joint local rules and rates the row "high for those districts". The rule is headed "Local Civil Rule 33.3. Interrogatories (Southern District Only)". A user in the Eastern District reading the row would apply a sequencing restriction that does not bind there.
2. Line 22, NDCA-RULES, says "Rule 37-1 meet-and-confer coverage includes discovery, preservation/spoliation, subpoenas, and sanctions". Civil L.R. 37-1(a) bars "a request or a motion to resolve a disclosure or discovery dispute" unless counsel have conferred, and does not mention preservation, spoliation or subpoenas. The list comes from the Commentary to Civil L.R. 37-3, which defines "Discovery-related motions" for the rule that such motions may not be filed more than 7 days after the discovery cut-off.
3. Line 23, NDCA-JUDGE, says the standing order "Requires disclosures about search/collection processes such as terms, repositories, and searchers in the specified judge's cases". The order says that "When the parties’ discovery dispute concerns the adequacy of a search or production, it is the Court’s expectation that the producing party disclose" the material aspects of its search and collection process. The row drops the condition and states the expectation as an unconditional requirement.
4. Line 24, SDFL-RULES, links the compilation "Revised December 1, 2023" and dates the row "Effective December 1, 2023 compilation". The court's local rules page lists "Local Rules - Effective December 1, 2025" as the current compilation, the words "Local Rules" linking to the 2025 file.
5. Line 25, SDFL-HANDBOOK, labels its link "S.D. Fla. Discovery Handbook" and rates it "Local guidance; medium/high for use with its court". The linked file is headed "DISCOVERY OBJECTIONS AND PROCEDURES FOR MAGISTRATE JUDGE LAUREN F. LOUIS" and states that its rules and procedures "apply in all cases assigned to United States Magistrate Judge Lauren F. Louis". The row presents one judge's procedures as district-wide guidance. The linked file is also not the judge's current document: her page on the court's site links "Discovery Procedures" to MJLDiscovery.pdf, headed "DISCOVERY PRACTICES AND PROCEDURES FOR MAGISTRATE JUDGE LAUREN F. LOUIS" and served with HTTP Last-Modified April 28, 2026, and does not link the file the row names, which is served with Last-Modified July 24, 2019.

## Objections considered
- Objection: The row's third statement says vague, incomplete or unexplained business/legal classifications 'can fail'. That is a general proposition, and the substituted opinion still supports it (the log standard at p. 9, the conclusory 'legal' label at p. 8, waiver for continual failure at p. 9), so nothing in the row is unsupported.
  Answer: In part. The general propositions survive as statements of the standard; what is gone is their application to BDO's log, while the row links, dates and rates ('high for holding') the withdrawn opinion. The proposed row keeps the log-information statement and the conclusory-label statement.
- Objection: The proposed row is itself inaccurate: 'describing advice as "legal" without more is conclusory' is attributed to the Fifth Circuit as a holding.
  Answer: The column is headed 'Evidence used', not holdings; the opinion states at p. 8 that 'courts have stated that simply describing a lawyer’s advice as "legal," without more, is conclusory and insufficient', and relies on it. The row's last sentence says the opinion does not rule on the log.
- Objection: The link still resolves and the withdrawn opinion reaches the same disposition (vacate and remand), so the harm is slight and line 114 already calls BDO a variation example.
  Answer: The court's own order withdraws the opinion; a withdrawn opinion is not citable authority for its holdings, and the linked file gives no notice. Line 114 concerns circuit variation, not the withdrawal.
- Objection: The SDNY-EDNY row only lists what the joint rules contain, and the joint rules do contain Rule 33.3.
  Answer: The row rates its contents "high for those districts", and the file at line 62 calls the rule "SDNY/EDNY Rule 33.3". The rule's own heading confines it to the Southern District; the added words say so and change nothing else.
- Objection: Civil L.R. 37-1 is the conferral rule for every discovery dispute, so it does cover spoliation, subpoena and sanctions disputes.
  Answer: Rule 37-1(a) covers "a disclosure or discovery dispute"; whether a particular spoliation or sanctions motion is such a dispute is not stated in it. The row's list is the text of the Commentary to Rule 37-3, which serves a different rule (the filing deadline after the cut-off). The replacement states what each provision says and attributes the list to the provision that contains it.
- Objection: An "expectation" in a standing order operates as a requirement in that judge's cases.
  Answer: The replacement keeps the order's force for the judge's cases; what it restores is the condition, a discovery dispute about the adequacy of a search or production. The row as written tells the user that search disclosures are required in every case before that judge.
- Objection: The SDFL-RULES row already says "recheck currentness" and "The compilation may have later amendments".
  Answer: The caveat does not cure a link and date that name a superseded compilation as the source; a user who follows the link reads the 2023 text. The provisions the row describes are the same in the 2025 compilation, so only the link and the date change.
- Objection: The SDFL-HANDBOOK row says the "Handbook is not a national rule and may not bind every judge", which is enough warning.
  Answer: The caveat assumes a district-level handbook; the linked file is one magistrate judge's procedures and applies only in cases assigned to her, and her page on the court's site links a later document in its place. The replacement links the document the judge's page links, names it by its heading and states its reach.
