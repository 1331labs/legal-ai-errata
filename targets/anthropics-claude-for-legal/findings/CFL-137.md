## Defect
Step 2 (Tier) of the AI inventory skill tells the model to "Check in order:" (line 132) and gives summaries of Article 5 (step A, lines 138 to 150) and Annex III (step B, lines 159 to 174) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, followed by the limited tier (step D, lines 186 to 188).

Annex III point 1 lists three biometric uses as high-risk where permitted by law: remote biometric identification, biometric categorisation according to sensitive or protected attributes or characteristics, and emotion recognition. Step B summarises area 1 as "Biometric identification and categorization" with no mention of emotion recognition (line 159), and step D assigns "emotion recognition and biometric categorization systems outside Article 5 scope" to limited risk with transparency obligations only (lines 186 to 188). Because the tiers are checked in order and emotion recognition is absent from step B, an emotion recognition system outside the workplace and education prohibition falls through to step D and is recorded as `limited`. The same happens to biometric categorisation according to sensitive or protected attributes or characteristics if the reader follows step D instead of the one-word mention in step B. The Article 50(3) duty to inform exposed persons applies in addition to high-risk classification, not in place of it (Article 50(6)).

The other step B summaries depart from Annex III as follows. Area 6 lists "deepfake detection", which Annex III point 6 does not list, and omits the risk of a person becoming a victim of crime (point 6(a)) and the assessment of personality traits or past criminal behaviour (point 6(d)). Area 7 lists "travel document verification", which point 7(d) expressly excludes ("with the exception of the verification of travel documents"), and omits polygraphs and detecting, recognising or identifying persons (points 7(a) and 7(d)). Area 8 lists "research and interpretation" without the condition that the system assist a judicial authority or be used in a similar way in alternative dispute resolution (point 8(a)), and omits the exclusion in point 8(b) for tools to whose output natural persons are not directly exposed. Area 2 omits the condition that the system be a safety component in the management and operation of the listed infrastructure. Area 4 lists "task allocation" without the condition in point 4(b) that tasks be allocated based on individual behaviour or personal traits or characteristics, and omits decisions affecting terms of work-related relationships. Area 3 omits point 3(c) (assessing the appropriate level of education) and drops "during tests" from point 3(d). Area 5 omits the fraud detection exception in point 5(b), the condition in point 5(a) that public assistance decisions be made by or on behalf of public authorities, and the call classification and patient triage limbs of point 5(d). Areas 1, 6 and 7 also omit "in so far as their use is permitted under relevant Union or national law". Areas 6 and 7 omit the users to which every point in them is confined: systems intended to be used by or on behalf of law enforcement authorities, or by Union institutions, bodies, offices or agencies in support of law enforcement authorities (point 6), and systems intended to be used by or on behalf of competent public authorities or by Union institutions, bodies, offices or agencies (point 7); area 6 also drops the limit of point 6(c) to evaluating the reliability of evidence "in the course of the investigation or prosecution of criminal offences".

The step A summaries depart from Article 5(1) as follows. Social scoring is limited to "public authorities" (line 141), but Article 5(1)(c) prohibits the placing on the market, putting into service or use of a social scoring system by any operator. The criminal-risk prohibition is summarised as "Predictive policing based solely on personality traits" (line 150), but Article 5(1)(d) also covers risk assessments based solely on profiling, and excepts systems that support a human assessment of a person's involvement in a criminal activity already based on objective and verifiable facts directly linked to a criminal activity. The scraping prohibition (line 149) omits "untargeted" and the purpose of creating or expanding facial recognition databases (Article 5(1)(e)). The manipulation and vulnerability prohibitions (lines 138 to 140) omit the significant-harm element of Article 5(1)(a) and (b); the first also omits "purposefully manipulative" techniques, the words "beyond a person’s consciousness" that confine the subliminal techniques, the requirement that the distortion work by appreciably impairing the ability to make an informed decision, and the requirement that it thereby cause the person to take a decision that they would not have otherwise taken, and the second states a purpose ("to materially distort behavior") where point (b) covers "the objective, or the effect" and gives "socio-economic status" where point (b) refers to "a specific social or economic situation". The social scoring summary also reduces the two kinds of detrimental or unfavourable treatment in Article 5(1)(c) to "detrimental treatment". The biometric categorisation prohibition (lines 144 to 146) omits the exception in Article 5(1)(g) for labelling or filtering lawfully acquired biometric datasets and categorising biometric data in the area of law enforcement. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (OJ L, 2026/1744, 24.7.2026; in force 27 July 2026) inserted points (ba) and (bb) into Article 5(1), prohibiting AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic non-consensual intimate material of an identifiable person and child sexual abuse material, with Article 5(1a) and (1b) limiting their scope; they apply from 2 December 2026 (new Article 113, third paragraph, point (a)). Step A lists neither.

Step D also omits two of the Article 50 transparency duties. Article 50(2) applies to providers of AI systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, and the second subparagraph of Article 50(4) to deployers of AI systems generating or manipulating text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Step D lists chatbots, deepfakes and the biometric systems only, so a text or image generator that is neither a chatbot nor a deepfake tool matches nothing before step E and is recorded as `minimal`.

The add flow makes the EU nexus a required field and states that "EU AI Act analysis applies" if the system is "deployed in the EU/EEA, offered to users in the EU/EEA, or used to produce outputs that affect people in the EU/EEA" (lines 83 to 85); the `eu_nexus` comment in the record example repeats the test (line 213). Article 2(1) does not turn on whether the output affects people in the Union. It reaches providers that place AI systems on the market or put them into service in the Union, deployers established or located in the Union, providers and deployers established or located in a third country where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union, and importers, distributors, product manufacturers and authorised representatives. A deployer established in the EU that uses a system only at its operations outside the EU, and a deployer outside the EU whose output is used in the EU in decisions about people outside it, are within Article 2(1) and answer no to every limb of the question; a deployer outside the EU whose output about EU residents is used only outside the EU answers yes although no limb of Article 2(1) reaches it. The question also treats Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway as within the Act. An act of the Union binds the EEA EFTA States only once an EEA Joint Committee decision incorporates it into the EEA Agreement (Articles 7 and 102 of that Agreement), and none had been adopted for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by 16 October 2026; the EFTA EEA-Lex database lists it as under scrutiny for incorporation.

The role step defines the product manufacturer as follows: "you put a general-purpose AI system (or another AI system) into a product under your own name/trademark. Treated as provider for the product." (lines 114 to 116). Article 2(1)(e) brings within the Regulation product manufacturers placing an AI system on the market or putting it into service together with their product and under their own name or trademark, and Article 25(3) treats a product manufacturer as provider only of a high-risk AI system that is a safety component of a product covered by the Union harmonisation legislation in Section A of Annex I, where that system is placed on the market together with the product, or put into service after the product has been placed on the market, under the manufacturer's name or trademark. The consequence is that of provider of the high-risk AI system, not of the product.

The role step defines the importer as a person who brings "an AI system into the EU from a provider established outside the EU" (lines 108 and 109). Article 3(6) defines the importer as a person located or established in the Union that places on the market an AI system bearing the name or trademark of a person established in a third country. A company in the EU that obtains a system from a provider outside the EU for its own use places nothing on the market and is a deployer, and a person outside the Union is not an importer. The step defines the authorised representative as a person who acts "on behalf of a non-EU provider" and is "established in the EU" (lines 112 and 113); Article 3(5) requires a written mandate from the provider, received and accepted, to perform and carry out on its behalf the obligations and procedures established by the Regulation, so a reseller or agent in the EU that acts for a provider in other respects is not one.

The record example at line 219 lists "FRIA if public body/service" among the deployer obligations and cites "Article 26"; the fundamental rights impact assessment is required by Article 27(1), and Article 26 does not contain it. Article 27(1) also requires it of deployers of the systems in Annex III points 5(b) and (c), whatever their status, so the condition "if public body/service" leaves out a credit scoring or life and health insurance pricing deployer. The condition also omits the limit of Article 27(1) to the high-risk AI systems referred to in Article 6(2), which are the Annex III systems, and its exception for systems intended to be used in the area listed in Annex III point 2 (critical infrastructure). Line 237 states that "the AI Act is phasing in through 2027"; Article 113, third paragraph, point (c)(ii), as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, applies Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3 to Annex I high-risk systems from 2 August 2028, and Article 111(1) and (2) set deadlines of 31 December 2030 for AI systems that are components of the large-scale IT systems established by the legal acts listed in Annex X and were placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2027, and of 2 August 2030 for providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems intended to be used by public authorities.

## Objections considered
- Objection: After Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 the Annex III high-risk rules, including Article 6 itself, do not apply until 2 December 2027, while Article 50(3) has applied since 2 August 2026. For an inventory filled in today the only operative obligation for an emotion recognition system outside Article 5 is transparency, so 'limited' is right.
  Answer: The skill's tiering is a classification of the system, not a statement of which obligations have started: step B records every other Annex III area as `high_risk` today with no reference to application dates, and the record carries a review date. A system recorded as `limited` now would not be picked up for conformity work before 2 December 2027. The correction states the classification without a date, like the rest of step B.
- Objection: Step B already says 'Biometric identification and categorization' and carries a '[verify against current AI Act text — Annex III]' tag, and the lists are labelled summaries; the model is expected to consult the text.
  Answer: Step B omits emotion recognition altogether, and step D then affirmatively assigns emotion recognition and biometric categorisation outside Article 5 to limited risk. The verify tags attach to steps A to C and to tier_basis; step D has none. With 'Check in order', an emotion recognition system matches nothing in B and matches D by name.
- Objection: The proposed text overstates: not every biometric categorisation system is high-risk, and an Annex III system can fall outside high-risk under Article 6(3).
  Answer: The corrected step D keeps biometric categorisation outside both Article 5 and Annex III point 1(b) in the limited tier, and moves only categorisation 'according to sensitive or protected attributes or characteristics' and emotion recognition. The Article 6(3) derogation is an exception a provider must assess and document, it never applies where the system profiles natural persons, and step B does not mention it for any Annex III area; it is left to the existing verify instruction rather than added for one area only.
- Objection: Step A is headed 'Summaries, not definitive text', so leaving out an element such as significant harm, or an exception, is a matter of brevity, not an error.
  Answer: A summary that adds a condition the Article does not contain (social scoring 'by public authorities') or drops one it does contain (profiling in point (d), 'untargeted' in point (e), significant harm in points (a) and (b)) decides the tier: with 'Check in order', a private social scoring system or a profiling-only crime risk tool matches nothing in step A, and a manipulative interface that causes no significant harm matches step A and is routed to the prohibited-practice workflow. The corrected lines stay one line of subject matter each and add only the element or exception that changes the match. The same applies to the Annex III areas whose summaries drop the condition that confines them, such as area 8, where a legal research tool for a law firm would otherwise be recorded as high-risk, and area 6, where an evidence-reliability tool used by defence counsel would.
- Objection: Points (ba) and (bb) of Article 5(1) do not apply until 2 December 2026, so a list of prohibited practices should not yet include them.
  Answer: The amending Regulation has been in force since 27 July 2026, and inventory entries carry a review date; a system recorded today without the prohibition would be in breach from 2 December 2026. The added line states that date, and refers to Article 5(1a) and (1b), which confine the provider prohibition to systems whose intended purpose is that generation or manipulation, or for which it is a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome without significant technical modification and which lack reasonable and adequate technical safety measures and other safeguards, and the deployer prohibition to use for that purpose.
- Objection: Lines 219 and 237 are an example record and a statement of design rationale, not tiering rules.
  Answer: The example record is the template the skill writes from, and its `obligations_note` is the text a lawyer reads when asked what a deployer must do; sending the reader to Article 26 for the impact assessment points to a provision that does not contain it. Line 237 gives the reason the skill does not derive obligations from a table, and it states an end date for the phase-in that the Regulation, as amended in July 2026, no longer has.
- Objection: The EU nexus question is a screen, and a screen should be generous; Article 2(1)(g) also extends the Regulation to affected persons located in the Union, so a system whose output affects people in the EU has a nexus.
  Answer: The question does not only over-include. A deployer established in the EU that uses a system only outside the EU, and a deployer outside the EU whose output is used in the EU about people elsewhere, are within Article 2(1)(b) and (c) and answer no, so the system is recorded without EU nexus and is never classified. Article 2(1)(g) concerns the rights of affected persons, such as those under Articles 85 and 86; it does not make an operator with no other connection to the Union subject to the role and tier obligations the inventory records.
- Objection: The AI Act is marked as text with EEA relevance and will be incorporated into the EEA Agreement, so treating the EEA as within its reach is prudent.
  Answer: An act of the Union binds Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway only through an EEA Joint Committee decision (Articles 7 and 102 of the EEA Agreement), and none had been adopted for this Regulation by 16 October 2026. The corrected question keeps those countries in view and asks for the incorporation status to be checked, so it does not need changing when a decision is adopted, and it does not tell a user with a nexus only to Norway that the Regulation applies today.
- Objection: The product manufacturer line is a short role label, and a manufacturer that embeds a third-party AI system under its own brand is usually a provider anyway.
  Answer: A manufacturer that embeds a system it did not develop or have developed is a provider only through Article 25: under paragraph 3 for a high-risk safety component of an Annex I Section A product, and under paragraph 1 where, among other cases, it puts its name or trademark on a high-risk AI system already on the market. The file's line makes every such manufacturer the provider of the product whatever the system, so a general-purpose chatbot in a household appliance is recorded as a provider role with provider obligations. The corrected line keeps the role, states the Article 25(3) case and sends the rest to the provider test and the dual-role flag.
- Objection: "Bring an AI system into the EU" is a plain rendering of importing, and the role list is a short guide.
  Answer: The role decides which obligations the inventory records. Article 3(6) turns on placing on the market by a person located or established in the Union, so a company in the EU that licenses a system from a provider outside the EU for its own use, which the line makes an importer, is a deployer; the corrected lines keep one sentence each and add only the conditions that change the match.
- Objection: Open pull request 85 already makes these corrections.
  Answer: Pull request 85 (opened 2026-06-02, open on 2026-10-21) changes three of the lines corrected here: social scoring, predictive policing and Annex III point 6. Its predictive-policing line omits the Article 5(1)(d) exception for systems that support a human assessment of a person's involvement in a criminal activity already based on objective and verifiable facts directly linked to a criminal activity, and it leaves the other corrections above unmade.
