## Defect
The California plausibility band used by the legal clinic deadline tracker has a single row for civil appeals: about 60 days after notice of entry, citing rule 8.104 (legal-clinic/references/plausibility-bands/CA.md, line 19). Rule 8.104 sets the time to appeal for cases other than limited civil cases (rule 8.100(a)(1)). Appeals in limited civil cases go to the appellate division under a separate chapter (rule 8.820) and must be filed on or before the earliest of 30 days after service of notice of entry or a filed-endorsed copy of the judgment by the clerk or a party, or 90 days after entry of judgment (rule 8.822(a)(1)). A limited civil case is one in which, among other conditions, the amount in controversy does not exceed $35,000 (Code Civ. Proc. § 85(a)), and it includes an unlawful detainer in which the damages claimed are $35,000 or less (§ 86(a)(4)). The deadlines skill says nothing when a date is inside the band and stops the student when it is outside by a material margin, so a student who enters a date 60 days out for a limited civil appeal receives no warning although the time to appeal ran out 30 days earlier, and a late notice of appeal must be dismissed (rule 8.822(d)). A student who enters the correct 30 day date is outside the band and is sent to rule 8.104 to recompute. The row also omits the outer limit that runs from entry of judgment (180 days under rule 8.104, 90 days under rule 8.822).

Line 16 gives the unlawful detainer response a band of "~10-14 calendar days after service". Since AB 2347 (Stats. 2024, ch. 512, effective January 1, 2025) the response is due within 10 days "excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (CCP § 1167(a)). Counting under CCP § 12, 10 such days after service on a weekday end 14 calendar days later, after service on a Saturday 13 days later and after service on a Sunday 12 days later; each judicial holiday in the period adds a day, and § 1167(b) adds five court days in the cases it lists. A student who counts 10 calendar days enters a date inside the band and the skill says nothing, while a correct date pushed past 14 days by judicial holidays or by § 1167(b) falls outside it.

Line 24 cites CCP § 116.710 for the small claims appeal period. Section 116.710 says who may appeal. The 30 day period is in § 116.750(b), and it runs from the date the clerk "delivered or mailed" notice of entry, not only from mailing. The note "limited de novo scope" does not say what is limited: the appeal is a new hearing (§ 116.770(a)) whose scope includes the claims of all parties who were parties when the notice of appeal was filed (§ 116.770(d)), and an appeal from the denial of a motion to vacate is confined to that denial (§ 116.730(e)).

Line 26 gives "~5 calendar days" for an unlawful detainer post-judgment motion to stay, citing CCP § 918. Neither § 918 nor any other cited provision sets a period within which a stay must be sought. Section 918(b) limits how long a trial court may stay enforcement without the adverse party's consent where an undertaking would be required on appeal; a stay pending appeal in unlawful detainer is governed by § 1176(a), which provides that the defendant's appeal does not stay the judgment automatically, that the petition is first directed to the trial judge, and that any stay is conditioned on payment of the monthly rental value into court. A five-day band for a stay motion flags any stay date that does not happen to fall five days after an event the row does not name.

Line 32 gives the federal answer as "~21 days after service (60 / 90 if waived)". Under FRCP 12(a)(1)(A)(ii) a defendant that has timely waived service under Rule 4(d) must answer within 60 days after the request for a waiver was sent, or within 90 days after it was sent to a defendant outside any judicial district of the United States. The row, which names service as the trigger, gives a student who counts from the date the signed waiver came back no reason to recount, although that date is later than the rule allows. The row also omits Rule 12(a)(2) and (3): the United States, a United States agency, and a United States officer or employee sued only in an official capacity answer within 60 days after service on the United States attorney, and an officer or employee sued individually for an act or omission in connection with federal duties within 60 days after the later of service on the officer or employee and service on the United States attorney, so a correct date for any of them is outside the 21 day band and is stopped, and the Notes column, whose citation the stop message tells the student to re-check against (legal-clinic/skills/deadlines/SKILL.md line 78), gives no 60 day period for them. Line 34 gives 60 days to appeal "if US is a party", but FRAP 4(a)(1)(B) gives every party 60 days where a party is the United States, a United States agency, a United States officer or employee sued in an official capacity, or a current or former United States officer or employee sued in an individual capacity for an act or omission in connection with duties performed on the United States' behalf; a correct 60 day date in a case against an agency or an officer is stopped by the 30 day band, and the Notes confine the 60 day period to cases in which the United States itself is a party. The correction leaves the 21 day and 30 day ranges in place and states the 60 day periods in the Notes, so a student whose correct date is stopped finds the period there and can confirm the date, which the stop message allows ("If your calculation is correct (local rule exception, atypical triggering event, tolling, waiver), confirm and I will add the entry as-is.").

## Objections considered
- Objection: The band file says its ranges are rough and that every citation is '[model knowledge — verify]', and the row tells the student to confirm the triggering event, so the row is a prompt to check, not a statement of the rule.
  Answer: The disclaimer goes to accuracy in general and the note goes to the trigger, neither to which rule applies. The deadlines skill acts on the band mechanically: line 76 writes the entry and says nothing when the date is inside the range, and line 77 stops the entry when it is outside by a material margin. A date 60 days out in a limited civil appeal is inside the only available band, so the check passes a date 30 days past the jurisdictional limit, and rule 8.822(d) requires dismissal.
- Objection: The row is meant for unlimited civil appeals to the Court of Appeal, which is what rule 8.104 governs; limited civil appeals are simply not covered, and step 5 of the skill handles types with no band.
  Answer: The row is labelled 'Notice of appeal (CA civil)' with no limit, and nothing else in the file or skill covers limited civil appeals, so a limited civil notice of appeal matches this row and not the no-band branch. The first California row of the same file is unlawful detainer, which is a limited civil proceeding when the damages claimed are $35,000 or less (CCP 86(a)(4)).
- Objection: Rows that allow extensions only under CRC 8.108 would ignore rule 8.66 and the statutes and rules 8.702 and 8.712 named in rule 8.104(a)(1), and a limited civil case is not defined by 'amount claimed' alone.
  Answer: The proposed rows say that rule 8.108 or 8.823 'can extend the time after certain post-judgment motions and for a cross-appeal' without claiming exclusivity, state the 180 and 90 day figures as the outer limits under rules 8.104(a) and 8.822(a), and refer to CCP 85 and 86 for classification ('generally $35,000 or less in controversy').
- Objection: "~10-14 calendar days" is an approximation, and a band should be generous at the edges.
  Answer: The likeliest error after AB 2347 is counting 10 calendar days instead of 10 court days, and a band that starts at 10 accepts that date. The corrected band starts at 12, the shortest count § 1167(a) can produce, and states the events that lengthen it, so a correct date is not stopped and a calendar-day count is.
- Objection: A clinic needs some deadline for an unlawful detainer stay, and five days reflects the time before a lockout.
  Answer: The five days before removal run from service of the writ of possession (CCP § 715.020(c)), not from judgment or from any event the row names, and they are a period for the levying officer, not a filing deadline for the tenant. The corrected row keeps that figure in the Notes column and says what it governs, outside the range column against which the skill compares dates, and cites § 1176(a) for the stay pending appeal.
- Objection: The federal table is general reference, and a clinic answering a federal complaint will read Rule 12 itself.
  Answer: The deadlines skill applies the federal rows mechanically, like the California rows. A 60 day answer date for a United States agency, or a 60 day notice of appeal in a case against a federal officer, is outside the band and is stopped, and the student is sent to Notes that give no 60 day answer period for the United States and confine the 60 day appeal period to cases in which the United States itself is a party; a waiver answer counted from the date the signed waiver came back is waved through under a row that names service as the trigger.
