## Defect
blb-workflows/spa/spa-samples.csv holds the gold extraction for ten stock purchase agreements distributed in blb-workflows/spa/documents. Forty-seven cells in the ten rows state something the cited agreement does not say. An extraction that reports the agreement correctly disagrees with the gold value in each of these fields, and in several cells an extraction that repeats the gold error agrees with it.

1. Durata, Effective Date (line 673): 2011-12-11. The agreement is dated as of December 11, 2009 on its cover and in its preamble, defines the Closing Date as December 18, 2009 and has a final termination date of December 30, 2009.
2. Durata, Company (line 673): "Vicuron PharmaceuticalsInc." The agreement names Vicuron Pharmaceuticals Inc.
3. Durata, Buyer Basket (line 690): "N/A". Section 10.2.2 applies the Threshold Amount, a tipping basket whose figure is redacted in the filed exhibit, to the Buyer's indemnity for its representations and warranties and for covenants to be performed prior to Closing (Sections 10.2.1(a) and (b)).
4. Durata, Buyer Survival Period (line 685): the cell gives the Buyer's indemnity the statute-of-limitations survival of "environmental matters and taxes (Sections 3.11, 3.13)" and lists capital structure and non-contravention as Buyer fundamentals. Those are Seller representations; the Buyer's representations that survive without limit are Sections 5.1, 5.2, 5.4(e) and 5.6, and the statute-of-limitations period in Section 10.3(b) otherwise applies to claims based upon fraud.
5. HealthEquity, Seller Liability Cap (line 50): "For breach of Fundamenal Reps: $50M". Section 5.2(d) limits recovery for breaches of the Company Fundamental Representations to an amount equal to the Purchase Price actually received by the Sellers. The Purchase Price is the Final Consideration plus any Escrow Amount released to the Sellers; $50,000,000 is the Base Consideration from which the Closing Consideration is computed under Section 2.4.
6. Deutsche Telekom, Seller Liability Cap (line 86): "For breach of reps: $500M" and "For losses in connection with Excluded Liabilities: $9,750,000,000". $500,000,000 is the Threshold in Section 6.4(a)(i), not a cap; $9,750,000,000 is the Seller Cap on Damages under Section 6.2(a)(i) (representations and the Section 4.16 covenant). No cap is stated for Excluded Liabilities.
7. Deutsche Telekom, Seller Basket (line 87): the $500,000,000 Threshold is missing (the Buyer basket cell of the same row records it); and the line "For breach of Section 3.2(f), 3.2(k), 3.2(o) and 3.2(p): $25M ((but each individual claim ... must exceed $1M individually)" states a $1M per-claim floor for Section 3.2(f), where the De Minimis Amount is $5,000,000, and does not reflect the Section 6.4(a)(iv) rule for Section 3.2(k).
8. Deutsche Telekom, Seller Survival Period (line 85): all representations other than the Fundamental Seller Representations are given 12 months. Section 6.1(a) gives Sections 3.2(k) and 3.2(s) three years and Section 3.2(l) the statute of limitations plus 30 days.
9. Deutsche Telekom, Buyer Basket (line 85): the $500M tipping basket is subject to a per-claim floor of "$1M individually or in the case of claims related to 3.2(f), 3.2(k) or 3.2(n), $5M individually". Sections 3.2(f), 3.2(k) and 3.2(n) are representations of Seller. Purchaser indemnifies under Section 6.2(b)(i) only for the failure of a representation or warranty made by Purchaser, and Section 6.4(b) applies to those claims "the De Minimis Amount" and the Threshold, with no rule for any Seller representation; the limitations do not apply to the Fundamental Purchaser Representations, which the cell does not state.
10. Deutsche Telekom, Fees and Expenses (line 114): the cell says the Company advisors' fees paid by Seller are reimbursable by Purchaser. Section 4.4 makes those fees Seller's; the reimbursement covers only Seller's out-of-pocket expenses under Section 4.8, Seller's Cooperation with Financing.
11. Deutsche Telekom, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 106, 109): each breach right is stated for "any breach of a representation or warranty" that "is not curable". Sections 7.4 and 7.3 give the right for a breach of any representation, warranty, covenant or agreement of the other party, and only where the breach or untrue representation means that Section 5.2(a) or 5.2(b) (Purchaser's right) or Section 5.3(a) or 5.3(b) (Seller's right) would not be satisfied. Each cell also gives a right on "any Order permanently restraining, enjoining or otherwise prohibiting consummation of the Transaction"; Section 7.2(b) gives it only once such an Order has become final and non-appealable.
12. Deutsche Telekom, Effect of Termination (line 112): the Termination Transfer is said to follow termination "pursuant to Section 7.2 or 7.3". Section 7.5(b) applies to a termination by Seller under Section 7.3 only where it follows an intentional failure in bad faith by Purchaser to comply with its obligations under Section 4.6.
13. Harsco, Buyer Basket, Seller Liability Cap and Seller Basket (lines 152, 163, 165): the Deductible, the Seller Environmental Deductible and the $2,312,500 and $23,125,000 caps are each described as applying "for any one rep" or "for any one specific rep". Section 9.03 measures each in the aggregate across all breaches. The Seller Basket cell also applies the $100,000 Mini-Basket to every individual claim; Section 9.03(a)(i) excludes the representations and warranties in Section 3.19 (Taxes) from it.
14. Fresh Express, Base Purchase Price (line 321): "$307,800,000". The defined Purchase Price is $307,800,000 less two times the Target Insurance Policy Costs ($15,000,000), that is $292,800,000.
15. Fresh Express, Conditions Precedent (line 354): the block headed "Buyer:" lists the Section 7.01 conditions to the obligations of the Seller and the Company, and the block headed "Seller:" lists the Section 7.02 conditions to the obligations of the Purchaser. In every other row that carries condition headings (the HealthEquity cell is "N/A"), the heading names the party whose obligation is conditioned.
16. National Financial Services, Buyer Basket and Seller Basket (lines 428, 433): "$3 million tipping basket". Section 6.2.4 makes the indemnitor liable only for amounts in excess of each $3.0 million basket, which is a deductible.
17. RTL, Conditions Precedent (line 520): the Buyer list omits the no Material Adverse Effect condition in Section 8.02(d), and each list states the bring-down as "Representations and warranties are true and correct". Section 8.02(a) tests the Seller's representations and warranties without their materiality qualifiers and with only such exceptions as would not reasonably be expected to have a Material Adverse Effect, and Section 8.03(a) tests the Buyer's in the same way by reference to a Buyer Material Adverse Effect.
18. Brady, Purchaser (line 572): "Brady Worldwide, Inc." Brady Worldwide, Inc. is Parent; the shares are sold to BW Acquisition Corp. ("Acquisition Sub").
19. Brady, Number of Shares and Pro Forma Ownership Percentage (lines 572, 593): the cells say all issued and outstanding shares are purchased, giving 100%. The Purchased Shares are the Sellers' approximately 99%; the balance is acquired through a merger under Section 253 of the DGCL on the following Business Day.
20. Brady, Seller Liability Cap (line 600): the cell gives only the per-Seller cap and omits the limit of aggregate Losses under Sections 9.02(a), 9.02(b) and 9.03(a) to the Indemnification Escrow Funds ($10,000,000 Indemnification Escrow Amount).
21. TreeHouse, Purchaser (line 743): "373978 B.C. ULC". The agreement names 1373978 B.C. ULC.
22. TreeHouse, Buyer and Seller Liability Cap (line 795): the Section 8.1(d) limit "in no event shall TreeHouse be liable for any amount under this Agreement in excess of the Purchase Price" is recorded as a limit on the Buyer, and the Seller cap is "N/A". TreeHouse is the seller.
23. TreeHouse, Termination Rights by Buyer (line 810): the Termination Date right is said to be unavailable if the failure is caused "by Buyer's failure to close the Debt Financing". Section 9.1(c) says the opposite. The breach right omits the condition in Section 9.1(b)(ii) that the Buyer not be in material breach of its obligations.
24. TreeHouse, Termination Rights by Seller (line 816): the breach right carries a clause "(b) by the Buyer (if Buyer is not in material breach ...)", which is the Buyer's right under Section 9.1(b)(ii), gives 30 days to cure where Section 9.1(b)(i) gives three Business Days for a failure to deliver the consideration due at the Closing, and omits the condition in Section 9.1(b)(i) that TreeHouse not be in material breach of its obligations. The Termination Date right is withheld "as long as the failure is not caused by Seller"; Section 9.1(c) withholds it only where the terminating party's failure to fulfill an obligation, constituting a breach, was the principal cause of the failure to close.
25. Harsco, Seller Survival Period (line 155): the three-year period is given to "Seller Fundamental Representations and 4.01, 4.02, 4.07 and 4.09". Sections 4.01, 4.02, 4.07 and 4.09 are representations of Buyer (Article IV), which Section 9.01 names the Buyer Fundamental Representations; Seller does not indemnify for them.
26. Harsco, Conditions Precedent (line 166): the Article III bring-down is stated as "correct without giving effect to materiality obligations", without the Section 8.02(b)(iii) exception for failures that have not had and would not reasonably be expected to have a Material Adverse Effect, and the Article IV bring-down as "correct without regard to materiality", without the Section 8.03(b) exception for failures that would not reasonably be expected to materially interfere with, prevent or materially delay Buyer's ability to perform or to consummate the transactions.
27. Harsco, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 186, 189): the breach right is stated for a breach of a representation only and without the cure period or the condition that the terminating party not then be in material breach; Sections 10.01(c) and 10.01(d) include breaches of covenants and agreements and impose both. The Outside Date right omits the Section 10.01(e) exclusion of a party whose failure to comply with its obligations is the primary cause of the failure to close, which the cell states for the right based on a final action.
28. BurgerFi, Seller Survival Period (line 266): "tax matters in 3.18 and 3.19". Section 8.01 extends survival for "Section 3.18 and Section 3.19 (solely insofar as it relates to Taxes)". Section 3.18 (Environmental Matters) contains no tax matter and survives in full; the Taxes limitation, stated in the singular, applies to Section 3.19 (Employee Benefit Matters).
29. BurgerFi, Conditions Precedent (line 271): the general bring-down for each party is "materially true and correct"; Sections 7.02(a) and 7.03(a) require the representations to be true and correct except where the failure would not have an ACFP Material Adverse Effect or a Buyer Material Adverse Effect. The Fundamental Representations are said to be true and correct "in all respects"; both sections except de minimis inaccuracies.
30. BurgerFi, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 291, 294): the January 31, 2022 right omits the exclusion in Sections 9.01(b)(ii) and 9.01(c)(ii) where the failure is due to the terminating party's failure to perform or comply with its covenants, agreements or conditions.
31. Fresh Express, Conditions Precedent (line 354): the Fundamental Representations must be true and correct "in all respects"; Section 7.02(a)(i)(1) excepts inaccuracies in Sections 4.04(a) and (b) that are de minimis in the aggregate.
32. Fresh Express, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 372, 375): the End Date right omits the Section 9.01(c) exclusion of a party whose failure to fulfill any obligation was the primary cause of the failure to close.
33. National Financial Services, Buyer Survival Period (line 424): the cell gives the Buyer's indemnity a period of 60 days after the statute of limitations for "certain representations and covenants". Section 6.2.1(b) gives that period to Seller representations (Sections 2.1.6, 2.1.13(i)-(t) and 2.1.15) and to the covenants in Sections 3.1.3(d) and 3.4(c); the Buyer's representations in Section 2.2 survive 18 months, or indefinitely for Section 2.2.1.
34. National Financial Services, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 461, 464): "Material breach on the part of Parent or Seller" and "of Purchaser". Sections 5.1(c) and 5.1(d) give the right for a material breach of covenants or a material failure to perform obligations, and only if the terminating side has performed and complied in all material respects with its own agreements and covenants.
35. RTL, Termination Rights by Buyer and by Seller (lines 538, 542): the Outside Date right omits the Section 10.01(b) exclusion of a party whose breach results in the failure to close; the injunction right omits the Section 10.01(c) condition of compliance with Section 5.05; the breach right omits the condition in Sections 10.01(d) and 10.01(e) that the terminating party not then be in material breach.
36. Brady, Closing Deliverables (line 572): "Option Termination Certificate". Section 2.04(a)(xi) requires Option Termination Agreements, executed by the Optionholders listed on Schedule 2.04(a)(xi).
37. Durata, Other Transaction Agreements (line 681): the cell lists the Dalbavancin Marketing Rights Agreement between Pfizer and RaQualia Pharma Inc. dated as of July 30, 2008, an existing contract. The Ancillary Agreement is the RaQualia Assignment Agreement between the Seller and the Buyer (Exhibit 1(g)), by which Pfizer assigns that contract to Durata.

## Objections considered
- Objection: "Effective Date" may mean something other than the execution date, such as closing.
  Answer: Closing was December 18, 2009, so no reading of the field gives 2011. The term Effective Date is defined in the Durata PDF only in exhibit assignment agreements, as December 18, 2009.
- Objection: The Deutsche Telekom $1M per-claim figure is right for Section 3.2(k).
  Answer: Section 6.4(a)(iv) counts 3.2(k) claims above $1,000,000.00 toward the Specified Deductible, but the $5,000,000.00 De Minimis Amount still bars liability for a smaller claim, and no such rule exists for Section 3.2(f). The replacement states the 3.2(k) rule and gives 3.2(f) its $5M floor.
- Objection: The Deutsche Telekom Buyer basket should state a per-claim figure, since the De Minimis Amount applies to Purchaser claims too.
  Answer: Section 6.4(b) applies "the De Minimis Amount" to claims under Section 6.2(b)(i), and Section 6.4(a)(ii) defines that term only by reference to representations and warranties of Seller; the agreement states no figure for Purchaser's representations. The replacement names the defined term without a figure, so an extraction that names the De Minimis Amount, or reads it as the $1,000,000.00 general figure, is not contradicted, while one that applies the $5M floor of Sections 3.2(f), 3.2(k) and 3.2(n), which are Seller representations, to the Buyer's indemnity is not matched.
- Objection: $307,800,000 is the headline price of the Fresh Express deal.
  Answer: The agreement defines the Purchase Price as $307,800,000 less $15,000,000. The replacement gives the defined amount and shows the headline figure and the deduction, so either statement of the price can be matched against it.
- Objection: Brady Worldwide, Inc. is the economic buyer.
  Answer: Parent pays at Closing (Section 2.03), but Section 2.01 transfers the Purchased Shares to Acquisition Sub. The replacement names both and their roles.
- Objection: "Tipping basket" is used loosely in practice for any threshold.
  Answer: The file uses "Tipping Basket" for the Deutsche Telekom Threshold, which makes Seller "liable for all such Damages" once it is exceeded, while the National Financial Services agreement makes the indemnitor liable only "in excess of" the basket. The two are different mechanisms and the gold must not record the same label for both.
- Objection: Swapping the Fresh Express headings is a matter of labelling convention.
  Answer: Every other row that carries condition headings, and schema.json ("conditions_to_sellers_obligations", "conditions_to_buyers_obligations"), use the heading to name the party whose obligation is conditioned. Under that convention the Fresh Express cell assigns each party's conditions to the other.
- Objection: $50M is the HealthEquity purchase price, so the fundamental-representations cap is stated correctly.
  Answer: Section 5.2(d) measures the limit by the Purchase Price actually received by the Sellers, which is the Final Consideration after the closing adjustments plus any released Escrow Amount; $50,000,000 is the Base Consideration only. The replacement names the measure and gives the base figure only as the starting point of the computation, so an extraction that states the measure is matched and one that states a fixed $50M cap is not.
- Objection: "3.18 and 3.19 (solely insofar as it relates to Taxes)" in the BurgerFi survival clause may limit both sections to Taxes.
  Answer: The qualifier is singular ("it relates"), and Section 3.18 (Environmental Matters) contains no statement about Taxes, so a Taxes limit on it would leave its reference in the proviso without effect. The replacement names both sections with their headings and attaches the Taxes limit to Section 3.19, as the text does; an extraction that repeats the clause verbatim is not contradicted, and one that limits the extended survival to tax matters is not matched.
- Objection: "Materially true and correct" is a common shorthand for a bring-down condition.
  Answer: The BurgerFi bring-down is measured by ACFP Material Adverse Effect and Buyer Material Adverse Effect, a different and higher threshold than materiality, and the Harsco cells state the representations without any exception at all. The replacements state the standard of each section, so an extraction that reports the MAE or ability-to-consummate standard is matched.
- Objection: A summary cell need not list every ground or condition of a termination right.
  Answer: The Deutsche Telekom and TreeHouse cells state each right in operative terms. The Harsco, BurgerFi, Fresh Express, National Financial Services and RTL cells likewise state breach, outside-date and injunction rights without the non-breach, performance, causation or compliance conditions on which the agreement makes them available, or (Harsco, National Financial Services) on a narrower or different ground than the agreement. An extraction that reports the covenant ground, the closing-condition link, the non-breach condition, the causation exclusion or the bad-faith trigger would otherwise disagree with a gold value that lacks it.
