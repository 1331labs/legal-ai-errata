## Defect
The third paragraph of the Term and Termination section (L62, referred to elsewhere in the template as Section 6.3) obliges the business associate to return or destroy PHI within sixty days only "upon receipt of a written request from Customer". If the customer sends no request, the template imposes no duty to return or destroy, and L58 keeps the BAA obligations running for as long as PHI is retained with no end point. The regulation requires a business associate contract to provide that the business associate will, at termination and if feasible, return or destroy all PHI it still maintains and retain no copies, and, if that is not feasible, extend the contract protections and limit further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible. The template therefore omits a required contract term, and a covered entity that signs it unchanged holds a business associate contract that does not meet the implementation specification. L58 supplies the extension of protections for retained PHI, but nothing in the template limits further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible, so the infeasibility alternative is only half stated. L62 is also triggered by termination of the Services Agreement or a downgrade, and does not mention expiration, although L58 speaks of "any termination or expiration".

The Term and Termination section (L56 to L62) also contains no provision authorizing Customer to terminate the BAA, and no other provision of the template does so. The regulation requires a business associate contract to authorize termination of the contract by the covered entity if the covered entity determines that the business associate has violated a material term. "Material" does not appear in the template, and "terminate" appears once, at L60, for the automatic end of Company's obligations after a downgrade; the other uses of "termination" (L56, L58, L62) describe the consequences of a termination, not a right to terminate. The BAA is an addendum to a Services Agreement whose non-conflicting terms remain in force (L69), but it neither refers to a termination right in that agreement nor makes a violation of the BAA a breach of it, so the template alone does not carry the required authorization.

Four other provisions that paragraph (e)(2) or § 164.314(a)(2) requires are missing or narrowed. (1) Use limit (L16). Clause (i) bars uses and disclosures "other than as permitted or required by this BAA or Applicable Law". Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(A) requires the contract to provide that the business associate will not use or further disclose the information "other than as permitted or required by the contract or as required by law", and § 164.502(a)(3) confines a business associate to uses and disclosures permitted or required by its contract "or as required by law". "Applicable Law" (L85) covers any national, state or local law, rule, regulation or regulatory guidance, so clause (i) authorizes any use or disclosure that some law permits. "Required by law" is itself a defined term of Part 164 (§ 164.103): a mandate in law that compels the use or disclosure and is enforceable in a court of law, a narrower class than "Applicable Law", which includes regulatory guidance. (2) Carrying out the customer's obligations. No provision states that, to the extent Company is to carry out an obligation of Customer under Subpart E of Part 164, it will comply with the requirements of Subpart E that apply to Customer in performing it, as paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(H) requires. (3) Secretary access (L24). Company makes its "documented internal practices and records" available to the Secretary only "to the extent necessary for Customer to satisfy its reporting and audit obligations to the Secretary". Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(I) requires the business associate to make its internal practices, books, and records relating to the use and disclosure of PHI available to the Secretary "for purposes of determining the covered entity's compliance with this subpart", a purpose the Secretary's review defines and the covered entity's own reporting obligations do not limit. (4) Subcontractors (L35). The subcontractor agreement need only pass through "substantially similar restrictions and obligations". Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(D) requires that subcontractors "agree to the same restrictions and conditions that apply to the business associate with respect to such information", and § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(B) requires that subcontractors handling electronic PHI agree to comply with the applicable requirements of Subpart C; L35 requires neither. Paragraph (e)(5) and § 164.314(a)(2)(iii) apply these requirements in the same manner where Customer is itself a business associate.

Two definitions in Appendix A misstate the law they refer to. "Security Incident" (L105) is defined as an unauthorized "access, use, disclosure, modification, transmission, or destruction, of PHI". Section 164.304 defines a security incident as "the attempted or successful unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction of information or interference with system operations in an information system", and § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) requires the contract to provide that the business associate will report "any security incident of which it becomes aware". L39 requires Company to report "any successful Security Incident to Customer, within the meaning of 45 CFR 164.304", and L42 gives standing notice of "unsuccessful Security Incidents". The L105 definition contains neither attempts nor interference with system operations, so it conflicts with the § 164.304 meaning that L39 invokes and leaves the successful and unsuccessful classes on which L39 and L42 rest without a defined content. L42 classes as "unsuccessful Security Incidents", for which the paragraph itself is the only notice, "denial of service attacks" and any "other security incidents that do not result in the unauthorized use, disclosure, modification, or destruction of Customer's PHI". Under § 164.304 a successful unauthorized access, and an interference with system operations in an information system, are security incidents whether or not PHI is then used, disclosed, modified or destroyed, and § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) requires them to be reported; L42 deems a successful denial of service attack, or an unauthorized access that goes no further, an unsuccessful incident noticed in advance, so for those incidents it contradicts the duty in L39 to report successful incidents within the meaning of § 164.304, and the template does not say which clause prevails. "Secure" (L103) states that the guidance issued under section 13402(h)(2) of the HITECH Act, "in the case of electronic information, requires that it be encrypted". The guidance (74 FR 42740, 42742 to 42743) treats PHI as unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable if electronic PHI has been encrypted as it specifies or if the media on which the PHI is stored have been destroyed, and for electronic media it accepts media "cleared, purged, or destroyed consistent with NIST Special Publication 800–88".

The reporting clause (L25) provides that, "in relation to all reporting obligations under this BAA", Company "will only be obligated to provide the limited device identifiers it Processes" when reporting on the identities of affected Individuals. Section 164.410(c)(1) requires a business associate's notification of a breach of unsecured PHI to include, "to the extent possible, the identification of each individual" affected, and paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(C) of § 164.504 requires the contract to provide for reporting of breaches "as required by § 164.410". Where Company can identify an affected Individual by more than a device identifier, L25 relieves it of the identification that § 164.410(c)(1) requires.

The breach report clause (L40) provides that "A Breach report will include the following information" and lists four items: a description of what happened with its dates, the identities or identifiers of the individuals affected, the data elements involved, and Company's efforts to investigate, mitigate and prevent recurrence; its next sentence, for information not available at the time of the Breach, refers back to "the information". Section 164.410(c)(2) also requires a business associate to provide the covered entity with "any other available information that the covered entity is required to include in notification to the individual under § 164.404(c)", at the time of the notification or promptly thereafter. The list omits that information, for example the steps individuals should take to protect themselves from potential harm resulting from the breach (§ 164.404(c)(1)(C)).

## Objections considered
- Objection: L58 already satisfies the regulation's alternative: Company's obligations survive for as long as it retains PHI, which is the 'extend the protections' limb.
  Answer: The regulation allows retention only where return or destruction is not feasible, and then requires two things: extension of the protections and a limit on further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible. The template makes retention the default whenever Customer sends no request, whether or not return or destruction is feasible, and nowhere limits further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible.
- Objection: Request-triggered deletion is a deliberate, processor-friendly drafting choice common in SaaS terms, and the Services Agreement or L20 (Company may delete or de-identify PHI) can cover deletion.
  Answer: Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(J) is an implementation specification that the contract 'must' meet; a commercial preference cannot displace it. L20 is a permission, not a duty, and the BAA controls over the Services Agreement on conflict (L69), so the required term has to be in the BAA. The correction keeps the processor-friendly elements: the sixty day period, destruction as the default, return only on request, and the infeasibility carve-out.
- Objection: The Services Agreement may already let Customer terminate for material breach, so a termination right in the BAA is unnecessary.
  Answer: The template cannot assume the content of the agreement it is attached to, and it neither refers to a termination right there nor makes a violation of the BAA a breach of the Services Agreement. Paragraph (e)(2)(iii) requires the business associate contract to authorize termination for violation of a material term; the added paragraph puts that authorization in the BAA and leaves any right under the Services Agreement in place (L69).
- Objection: Customer may itself be a business associate (L3: "as a Covered Entity or Business Associate"), in which case the covered-entity requirement does not apply.
  Answer: Paragraph (e)(5) applies the requirements of paragraph (e)(2) to a contract between a business associate and its subcontractor "in the same manner", so the termination authorization is required whichever capacity Customer holds.
- Objection: "Substantially similar" terms are the norm in commercial subcontracting and give the same protection.
  Answer: Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(D) requires "the same restrictions and conditions", and § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(B) requires a Security Rule undertaking that L35 does not mention. A subcontract that differs in substance from the BAA is not one the regulation accepts. The replacement leaves the trigger, the cited sections and the confidentiality language in place.
- Objection: L24 gives the Secretary access, and the limit protects Company from open-ended requests.
  Answer: Paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(I) fixes the purpose of the Secretary's access as determining the covered entity's compliance, and the contract may not narrow it to what the covered entity needs for its own reporting. The replacement keeps that limit for Customer's own access, where the regulation imposes none.
- Objection: Clause (i) of L16 was meant to cover disclosures required by law, and the template's other limits (ii) and (iii) contain it.
  Answer: Clauses (ii) and (iii) forbid unlawful and excessive uses; neither confines Company to uses the BAA permits. As written, clause (i) admits any use or disclosure that a law "permit[s]". The replacement confines clause (i) to uses and disclosures that the BAA permits or requires or that the law, as defined in § 164.103, requires, and changes nothing else.
- Objection: Company performs none of Customer's Privacy Rule obligations (L29 and L31), so paragraph (e)(2)(ii)(H) has nothing to apply to.
  Answer: The implementation specification is a term the contract "must" contain, conditioned in its own words on the extent of the delegation; the new clause (iv) is likewise conditioned and imposes nothing while Company carries out no such obligation.
- Objection: The "Security Incident" definition was narrowed on purpose to limit reporting to events affecting Customer's PHI.
  Answer: L39 itself reports Security Incidents "within the meaning of 45 CFR 164.304", and § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) requires the contract to provide for reporting of "any security incident". The narrowed definition conflicts with L39 and gives the successful and unsuccessful classes in L39 and L42 no content. Reporting of incidents involving Customer's PHI as "Incidents" is unaffected (L95).
- Objection: Standing notice of unsuccessful attempts is common practice, and a denial of service attack or an access that touches no PHI does no harm, so L42 should stand as written.
  Answer: The corrected parenthetical keeps the standing notice for every attempt that does not succeed. It moves to L39 only the incidents that § 164.304 defines as successful (unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification or destruction of information, or interference with system operations, in an information system), which § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) requires the business associate to report and which L39, by its reference to § 164.304, already undertakes to report.
- Objection: The "Secure" definition only describes how Company will secure retained PHI, and encryption is the only method that leaves the data usable.
  Answer: The definition attributes the encryption-only requirement to the guidance, which also accepts cleared, purged or destroyed electronic media. The replacement states the guidance as published; under L20 Company may still choose encryption.
- Objection: Paragraphs (e)(2)(ii)(E) and (F) require covenants to make PHI available for access and amendment, and the template has none.
  Answer: Both covenants operate "in accordance with" §§ 164.524 and 164.526, whose rights attach to PHI in a designated record set, and HHS's sample provisions mark them as not necessary where the business associate has no PHI in a designated record set. L26 records that Company maintains none, so the template needs no such covenant; a user whose business associate does maintain a designated record set must delete L26 and add them.
- Objection: The termination right should be subject to a cure period.
  Answer: Paragraph (e)(2)(iii) does not require one, and the added paragraph states only what the paragraph requires. A user who wants a cure period can add one; the template's other operative provisions are unaffected.
- Objection: Customer acknowledges in L25 that Company Processes no directly identifying information other than device identifiers, so device identifiers are all that is possible.
  Answer: Where that is so, the exception requires nothing more, since § 164.410(c)(1) asks for identification only "to the extent possible". It operates only where Company can identify an affected Individual by more, which the cap as drafted excuses.
- Objection: L40 already requires Company to report Breaches "in accordance with the requirements set forth in 45 CFR 164.400 et seq.", which includes § 164.410(c)(2).
  Answer: The next sentence states what "A Breach report will include" as a list of four items, and the sentence after it, for information that is not available at the time, refers back to "the information"; read together, they fix the report's content without the § 164.410(c)(2) information. Item (v) states that information in the regulation's words and leaves the general reference in place.
