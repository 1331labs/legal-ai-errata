## Defect
The task instructs the solver to draft a petition "complying with Rules 32 and 34", and the petition is the only deliverable. Criterion C-006 in tasks/tax/draft-tax-court-petition/task.json (title line 60, text line 64) credits the petition only if it sets out the employer identification number and fails it if the number is omitted. Rule 34(b)(1) lists the required contents of a deficiency petition in items (A) to (K) and none of them is a taxpayer identification number. Rule 27(a)(1) requires a filer to refrain from including, or to redact, taxpayer identification numbers, expressly including employer identification numbers, and Rule 20(b) requires the number to be submitted with the petition on a separate Statement of Taxpayer Identification Number (Form 4). A petition drafted to the Court's rules therefore fails C-006, and because the task is scored only when every criterion is met it receives no credit.

C-049 (title line 404, text line 408) fails a petition that does not designate a place of trial. Rule 140(a) requires the petitioner, when filing a petition, to "also file a separate paper requesting the place of trial" (Form 5); Rule 34(b)(1) does not list a place of trial among the contents of a petition, and Form 1 has no place for one. The petition is the only deliverable, so a petition that leaves the request to the separate Form 5 fails C-049.

C-055 (title line 452, text line 456) fails a petition that has no "verification, declaration, or similar attestation" and states that a verification is "required by Tax Court Rule 34(b)(7) for petitions filed by counsel". Rule 34(b) has three paragraphs, (1) to (3), and no paragraph (7). Rule 34(b)(1)(K) requires the signature, mailing address, email address and telephone number of the petitioner or counsel and counsel's Tax Court bar number, and neither Rule 34 nor Form 1 calls for a verification or a declaration under penalty of perjury. A petition in the form of Form 1, signed by counsel, fails C-055.

C-008 (title line 76, text line 80) credits the petition if it identifies counsel "with either her Tax Court Bar No. TN-4871 or Ohio Bar No. 0087412 (or both)" and fails it only if "no bar number is provided". Rule 34(b)(1)(K) requires "counsel’s Tax Court bar number", and the signature block of Form 1 has a line for "Counsel’s Tax Court Bar Number"; a state bar number is not that number. No task document states a Tax Court bar number: the only bar number in the record is the Ohio number in documents/appeals-case-memo.docx. A petition that gives only the Ohio number therefore passes C-008 although it omits an item Rule 34(b)(1)(K) requires, while a petition that leaves only a line or placeholder for the Tax Court bar number, the only course the record allows, and gives no other bar number, fails it. The criterion's other branch credits "Tax Court Bar No. TN-4871", which appears in no task document, so a petition meets that branch only by stating a number the record does not supply.

C-022 (title line 188, text line 192) credits the petition only if it alleges that the IRS wrongly found net § 1231 gains in the five preceding years "and therefore the § 1231(c) lookback recapture rule does not apply to convert the loss to capital", and fails a petition that does not contest the determination "on this basis". Section 1231(c)(1) treats a year's net section 1231 gain as ordinary income to the extent of the non-recaptured net section 1231 losses of the five preceding years; it has no application to a year with a net section 1231 loss, and under § 1231(a)(2) a net section 1231 loss is not treated as a loss from the sale or exchange of a capital asset. A petition that assigns error on the ground that § 1231(c) cannot recharacterize the 2021 loss whatever the prior years show states a complete answer to Adjustment 4(b) and fails C-022.

C-027 (title line 228, text line 232) credits an economic performance argument "under § 461(h)(2)(C) (payment as economic performance for breach-of-contract/tort liabilities) citing the $500,000 escrow deposit, or under the recurring item exception of § 461(h)(3), or both". The Archer-Hollis liability arises from "a breach of supply agreement" (documents/appeals-case-memo.docx). Section 461(h)(2)(C) reaches only workers compensation and tort liabilities; the payment rule for a liability arising out of a breach of contract, including the settlement of a dispute in which a breach of contract is alleged, is Treas. Reg. § 1.461-4(g)(2). Treas. Reg. § 1.461-5(c) provides that the recurring item exception does not apply to a liability described in § 1.461-4(g)(2), so a petition that rests economic performance on it alone passes C-027 on a ground the regulations exclude. Under Treas. Reg. § 1.461-4(g)(1)(i), except as provided in § 1.461-4(g)(1)(iv) (assignments) and § 1.461-6 (qualified assignments under section 130 and designated settlement funds under section 468B), a payment into an escrow account is economic performance only if it constitutes payment to the person to which the liability is owed; the criterion presents the escrow deposit as payment without that condition.

C-031 (title line 260, text line 264) credits the petition on the clean room adjustment only if it argues that the clean room, or the disputed $2,029,100, "is not a 'structural component' of the building but rather specialized manufacturing equipment qualifying as § 1245 property", and fails a petition that "does not argue that the clean room is process-related/manufacturing equipment rather than a structural component". The notice of deficiency classifies the disputed portion as nonresidential real property with a 39-year recovery period, ineligible for bonus depreciation. The revenue agent's report places the clean room "within and attached to the existing building structure", occupying part of Building C's interior, and the clean room was placed in service in September 2020. Under § 168(e)(6), as amended by section 2307 of the CARES Act with effect for property placed in service after December 31, 2017, an improvement made by the taxpayer to an interior portion of a building that is nonresidential real property, placed in service after the building was first placed in service, is qualified improvement property unless the expenditure is attributable to an enlargement, an elevator or escalator, or the internal structural framework; qualified improvement property is 15-year property under § 168(e)(3)(E)(vii) and so meets the recovery-period test of § 168(k)(2)(A)(i)(I). Treas. Reg. § 1.168(b)-1(a)(5)(i) also requires the improvement to be section 1250 property, the character the notice of deficiency gives the disputed portion by classifying it as nonresidential real property. The applicable percentage for property acquired after September 27, 2017 and placed in service in 2020 is 100 percent (§ 168(k)(6)(A)(i); Pub. L. 115-97, § 13201(h)(1)); for property acquired before September 28, 2017 and placed in service after 2019 it is 0 percent (§ 168(k)(8)(D)(i)). The record describes a clean room "constructed in 2020". A petition that assigns error on the ground that the wall panels, ceiling panels, flooring and vestibules are qualified improvement property eligible for 100 percent bonus depreciation challenges Adjustment 2 on a ground the statute supports, and fails C-031.

## Objections considered
- Objection: Rule 34(b)(1) also requires the petition to be substantially in accordance with Form 1, and the form may call for the identification number.
  Answer: Form 1 (Petition (Sample Format), in the Appendix to the Court's Rules of Practice and Procedure, printed pages 234 and 235 (PDF pages 237 and 238), read 2026-10-15) has no field for a taxpayer identification number. Its paragraph 2 says the attached notice of deficiency "should be redacted as provided by Rule 27" and that "Petitioner must submit with the petition a Form 4, Statement of Taxpayer Identification Number."
- Objection: Rule 27(g) lets a petitioner include its own EIN, so a petition with the EIN is a valid filing and the criterion is harmless.
  Answer: C-006 does not merely tolerate inclusion; it fails a petition that omits the number, which is the conduct Rule 27(a)(1) directs ("must refrain from including"). The waiver in Rule 27(g) describes the effect of an unredacted filing and creates no duty to include.
- Objection: The criterion tests extraction of a fact from the record, not Tax Court practice.
  Answer: The instruction sets the standard as a petition "complying with Rules 32 and 34", Rule 34(b)(1) lists the contents and omits any identification number, and under all-pass scoring a rule-compliant petition scores 0 on the task because of this one criterion.
- Objection: The inverted alternative (fail a petition that includes the EIN) penalises conduct Rule 27(g) permits, and deleting a criterion leaves a gap in the ids and changes the count.
  Answer: As to the alternative, the patch deletes C-006 and offers the inverted criterion only as an option, with the Rule 27(g) point stated. As to deletion, ids are already non-contiguous in 17 task files at head, tests/test_task_integrity.py requires only unique ids, and the CHANGELOG entry records the change in count.
- Objection: Many practitioners put the requested place of trial in the petition or its caption, so C-049 tests ordinary good practice.
  Answer: Rule 140(a) requires the request to be made in "a separate paper" filed with the petition, and Form 1 has no place for it. A petition that names a city is not penalised by the deletion; a petition that leaves the request to Form 5, as the rule directs, is no longer failed.
- Objection: C-055 accepts "an equivalent sworn statement" or "similar attestation", so counsel's signature block satisfies it.
  Answer: The criterion asks for "a verification or declaration under penalties of perjury" and states that Rule 34(b)(7) requires one for counsel's petitions; a judge applying that text looks for a sworn statement, which a signature block is not. The signature requirement is Rule 34(b)(1)(K), already tested by C-030 (ISSUE_008, counsel signature block), so rewriting C-055 to test the signature would duplicate that criterion.
- Objection: C-055 cites Rule 34(b) by the numbering the Court's own Form 1 still uses (paragraph 5 of Form 1 refers to "34(b)(5)").
  Answer: Under the numbering in force before March 20, 2023, Rule 34(b)(7) was the requirement of "The signature, mailing address, and telephone number of each petitioner or each petitioner’s counsel, as well as counsel’s Tax Court bar number", and Rule 33(a) then provided that pleadings "need not be verified or accompanied by affidavit or declaration" unless the Court directed otherwise (notice of proposed amendments, March 23, 2022). Under either numbering no rule calls for a verification; the signature, address, telephone, e-mail and bar number requirement is Rule 34(b)(1)(K) as amended effective March 20, 2023.
- Objection: The record does not give a Tax Court bar number, so C-008 rightly accepts the Ohio number.
  Answer: Rule 34(b)(1)(K) requires counsel's Tax Court bar number and Form 1 provides a line for it; a state bar number is not that number, and the instruction asks for a petition complying with Rule 34. The replacement credits a line or placeholder for it, which is how a drafter without the number meets the rule, and still allows the Ohio number as additional information.
- Objection: A petition that gives a Tax Court bar number may give counsel's real number, so failing it is too strict.
  Answer: No task document states a Tax Court bar number, and the petition is drafted from the task documents, so any number it gives is one the record does not support; the "TN-4871" in the shipped criterion appears in no document. A judge cannot tell a correct number from an invented one, and a petition that states an invented bar number misidentifies counsel to the Court.
- Objection: C-022 tests whether the solver catches the factual error about prior-year gains that the record plants, which is the point of ISSUE_003.
  Answer: The replacement keeps that route and still credits it. It adds the statutory route because § 1231(c)(1) applies only to net section 1231 gain, so a petition that assigns error on that ground answers Adjustment 4(b) completely, and the criterion's own sentence ("therefore the § 1231(c) lookback recapture rule does not apply to convert the loss to capital") states as law a conversion the statute does not make.
- Objection: The CPA memo asks for the § 461(h)(2)(C) escrow argument and the recurring item exception in the alternative, and C-027 follows the record.
  Answer: The memo is a task input, and the rubric's statement of the law must be correct whatever the inputs say. Treas. Reg. § 1.461-5(c) excludes the recurring item exception for a liability described in § 1.461-4(g)(2), which includes a liability arising out of the settlement of a dispute in which a breach of contract is alleged. The replacement still credits a petition that follows the memo, including its citation of § 461(h)(2)(C) and an alternative plea of the recurring item exception; it fails only a petition that rests economic performance on that exception alone.
- Objection: C-031 tests ISSUE_009, the manufacturing equipment position the client asks counsel to advance, and a petition that drops the client's position is deficient.

The replacement keeps the manufacturing equipment route and still credits it; it adds the qualified improvement property route because a petition resting on § 168(e)(6) contests the adjustment on a ground that, if the facts bear it out, allows 100 percent bonus depreciation on the whole $2,029,100 whether or not the components are structural.
