## Defect
Criterion C-041 in tasks/international-trade-sanctions/draft-export-license-application/task.json (title line 346, text line 350) credits the draft application narrative only if it cites Singapore's Country Group A:1 status as a favorable factor and fails a narrative that does not refer to A:1. In the Country Group A table of Supplement No. 1 to Part 740 of the Export Administration Regulations the Singapore row carries a single mark, in column A:6, both as of April 1, 2025 (the screening memo in the task is dated April 1, 2025) and in the current text; Singapore also appears in the Country Group B list. Footnote 1 to the table describes Country Group A:1 as the list of Wassenaar Arrangement Participating States, except for Malta, Russia and Ukraine. The deliverable is a narrative for filing with the Bureau of Industry and Security: a narrative that satisfies C-041 misstates the regulation to the agency, and an accurate narrative fails the criterion and, because every criterion must be met, the task.

Criterion C-002 (title line 28, text line 32) credits a memorandum that "states or explains that Myanmar is under comprehensive U.S. sanctions and/or is in Country Group D:1 and E:1". In the same Supplement the Burma row of the Country Group D table carries marks in columns D:1 (National Security), D:3 (Chemical & Biological) and D:5 (U.S. Arms Embargoed Countries) and none in D:2 or D:4, and Burma is not in the Country Group E table, whose only rows are Cuba (E:2) and Iran, North Korea and Syria (E:1). The OFAC Burma Sanctions Regulations, 31 CFR part 525, block the property of persons determined under Executive Order 14014 (§ 525.201(a)) and contain no general prohibition on exports to Burma, and part 746 of the EAR (embargoes and other special controls) has no Burma section. Each of the two statements C-002 offers as sufficient is therefore inaccurate as of April 1, 2025, and a memorandum that repeats either misstates the regulations to the client. C-002 also requires the memorandum to state that re-export of 6A002-controlled items to Myanmar "would be prohibited or subject to a policy of denial". On this record that clause is supportable and is kept. The modules are intended to be used by the Republic of Singapore Navy, a 'military end-user' as 15 CFR 744.9(d) defines the term, and Burma is outside Country Group A:1, so § 744.9(a)(1)(ii) requires a license for the re-export, and under § 744.9(c) the application is reviewed by applying the policies of the International Traffic in Arms Regulations, under which 22 CFR 126.1(d)(1) lists Burma among the countries that have a policy of denial for defense articles and defense services. Under 15 CFR 742.4(b)(7)(i) every application for Burma is reviewed for the risk of diversion to a military end user or military end use, with a presumption of denial for items that would make a material contribution to the weapons systems of Burma, and under 15 CFR 744.21(e) an application for a military end use in Burma or for a Burmese military end user is reviewed with a presumption of denial.

Criteria C-009 (line 89) and C-010 (line 98) make the § 744.21 military end-use license requirement turn on whether Singapore is in "Country Group D:1 through D:5 or E:1 or E:2". Section 744.21(a), as of April 1, 2025, names countries, not Country Groups: it reaches a 'military end use' in, or a 'military end user' of, Burma, Cambodia, China, Nicaragua or Venezuela (paragraph (a)(1)) and Belarus or Russia (paragraph (a)(2)). The conclusion that § 744.21 adds no license requirement for the Singapore end use and end user is right, but the reason both criteria require is wrong, and C-010 fails a deliverable that states the trigger correctly if "the Country Group analysis" is read as required.

Criterion C-009 also accepts a discussion of "Part 744 military end-use provisions generally" that concludes no additional license requirement arises, and C-034 (title line 288, text line 292) credits a narrative that discusses "Part 744 end-use and end-user controls" with "an affirmative statement that neither imposes additional restrictions on this transaction". Section 744.9(a)(1)(ii), as of April 1, 2025, requires a license, in addition to the license requirements for national security, regional stability and other reasons, if the exporter knows that commodities described in ECCN 6A002 or 6A003 (other than 6A003.a.3, a.4 and a.6) will be or are intended to be used by a 'military end-user' in any destination except those in Country Group A:1, and § 744.9(d) defines military end-user to include the national armed services, among them the navy. The purchase order, the screening memo and the end-use certificate name the Republic of Singapore Navy as the operational end-user, and Singapore is not in Country Group A:1, so § 744.9 requires a license for this export whichever of the two ECCNs applies, and § 744.9(e) bars every license exception except GOV for shipments to the U.S. Government. A deliverable that tells BIS that no Part 744 end-use or end-user control applies misstates the regulations and passes C-009 and C-034 as written, and a narrative that identifies the § 744.9 requirement alongside a correct § 744.21 and catch-all analysis is exposed to failure under C-034 for not stating that Part 744 imposes no additional restrictions. The conclusion C-009 and C-034 test for § 744.21 and the proliferation catch-alls is right; only the extension to Part 744 as a whole is wrong.

Criterion C-011 (line 107) gives as examples of the proliferation catch-alls "§ 744.3 chemical/biological, § 744.4 missile technology, or § 744.6 / General Prohibition 10". Section 744.3 restricts rocket system and unmanned aerial vehicle end uses, § 744.4 chemical and biological weapons end uses, and § 744.6 the activities of U.S. persons, which General Prohibition Seven (15 CFR 736.2(b)(7)) enforces; General Prohibition Ten (§ 736.2(b)(10)) concerns proceeding with a transaction with knowledge that a violation has occurred or is about to occur. A deliverable that follows the examples miscites the sections to BIS.

Criterion C-014 (line 131) states that "BIS Form BIS-748P requires disclosure of all parties to the transaction including freight forwarders (e.g., in Block 19 or equivalent field)". Under the form instructions in Supplement No. 1 to Part 748, Block 19 is the End-User block, completed only if the ultimate consignee is not the end user. Section 748.5 lists the parties that "may be entered on the application"; a forwarding agent appears there as a possible intermediate consignee (§ 748.5(d), Block 17), and Block 24 asks for "names of parties of interest not disclosed elsewhere". No block is designated for freight forwarders.

Criteria C-022 (line 195) and C-023 (lines 199 and 204) state that a BIS individual validated license has a "typical 2-year (24-month)" validity period. Section 750.7(g), as of April 1, 2025, provides that licenses "will generally have a four-year validity period, unless a different validity period has been requested and specifically approved by BIS or is otherwise specified on the license at the time that it is issued". The point decides the issue C-022 tests: the email chain anticipates follow-on orders of 12 to 18 modules over the next 36 months, which fall within a four-year validity period, so a memorandum that applies § 750.7(g) can reach the opposite conclusion from one that applies two years, and C-023 fails a deliverable that states the rule correctly.

Criteria C-026 (title line 224, text line 228), C-027 (title line 232, text line 236) and C-045 (title line 379, text line 384) require the deliverables to classify the CP-640IR under ECCN 6A002.a.3, and C-031, C-032 and C-033 (lines 268, 276 and 284) tie the reasons for control to that ECCN. The technical data sheet describes the CP-640IR as a 640×512 InSb focal plane array with "an integrated Stirling-cycle microcooler, cooler drive electronics, and a digital video output interface (Camera Link), providing a complete sensor core solution", in which "On-board electronics perform non-uniformity correction, bad-pixel replacement, and gain/offset adjustment in real time" and "The corrected digital video stream is output via the Camera Link interface". Note 1 to 6A003.b.4 of the Commerce Control List, as of April 1, 2025, provides that imaging cameras described in 6A003.b.4 include focal plane arrays combined with sufficient signal processing electronics, beyond the read out integrated circuit, to enable as a minimum the output of an analog or digital signal once power is supplied. On the data sheet the module is an imaging camera incorporating a focal plane array controlled by 6A002.a.3.c, described in 6A003.b.4.a, not a focal plane array in 6A002.a.3. C-026 and C-045 fail a deliverable that classifies the module under 6A003.b.4.a.

C-027 also offers as the classification basis "640×512 array exceeding 256 elements, 3–5 μm MWIR band, NETD of 18 mK below the 20 mK threshold", taken from the data sheet's statement that these parameters "exceed the control thresholds specified in ECCN 6A002.a.3". Neither 6A002.a.3 nor 6A003.b.4 contains an element-count threshold of 256 or any NETD threshold; 6A002.a.3.c covers non-"space-qualified" two-dimensional focal plane arrays whose individual elements have a peak response in the wavelength range exceeding 1,200 nm but not exceeding 30,000 nm. A narrative that follows the example states control parameters to BIS that the Commerce Control List does not contain.

C-032 requires the narrative to address "the Missile Technology (MT) reason for control for ECCN 6A002.a.3" and to explain why the export should be approved notwithstanding it. ECCN 6A003 carries no MT control (its reasons for control are NS, NP, RS, AT and UN), and under ECCN 6A002 MT applies only to optical detectors in 6A002.a.1 or a.3 that are specially designed or modified to protect missiles against nuclear effects and usable for missiles. Nothing in the record describes the CP-640IR as so designed (its EMI/EMC qualification is MIL-STD-461G RE102, CE102, RS103 and CS101). A narrative that concludes that MT does not apply fails C-032 as written, and one that passes repeats to BIS the screening memo's statement that MT applies to the item. NS and AT apply to the entire entry under both 6A002 and 6A003, so in C-031 and C-033 only the reference to ECCN 6A002.a.3 changes.

Criterion C-025 (title line 216, text line 220) credits a memorandum that concludes that no license exception applies by "specifically addressing that License Exception STA (§ 740.20) does not cover ECCN 6A002.a.3 items when MT reasons for control are present". The conclusion is right, but the stated reason makes the MT control the condition of the exclusion. Section 740.20(b)(2)(x), as of April 1, 2025, bars License Exception STA for every item controlled by ECCN 6A002, whether or not MT applies, and for a camera in 6A003.b.4 the STA paragraph of ECCN 6A003 bars STA to Country Group A:6 destinations, among them Singapore; § 740.20(b)(2)(iii) excludes items controlled for MT reasons, but on the record MT does not apply to the CP-640IR (see C-032). Section 744.9(e) also bars every license exception except GOV for shipments consigned to and for the official use of the U.S. Government.

Criterion C-020 (title line 175, text line 179) is titled "Flags insufficient documentation of Entity List near-match resolution". The screening memo records the partial match for "Stellar Defence Systems Pte Ltd" against the Unverified List, which it identifies as Supplement No. 6 to Part 744, and lists the Entity List separately as Supplement No. 4 to Part 744; the criterion's own text places the match "on the Unverified List". The title names a list on which no match was returned.

- Objection: A module sold without optics is a detector assembly, not an imaging camera, so 6A002.a.3 remains the better classification.
  Answer: Note 1 to 6A003.b.4 defines the cameras described in 6A003.b.4 by the electronics beyond the read-out integrated circuit and the output of a signal once power is supplied; it sets no optics requirement. On the data sheet the module digitizes in the read-out integrated circuit, processes the image in separate on-board electronics and outputs digital video over Camera Link from an 18–36 VDC supply. The replacement criteria still credit a deliverable that reports the record's 6A002.a.3 self-classification and flags 6A003.b.4.a for confirmation, and they accept the ECCN stated as 6A003, 6A003.b.4 or 6A003.b.4.a.
- Objection: C-025 reaches the right conclusion, so the MT wording does no harm.
  Answer: The criterion states the MT control as the condition on which STA is unavailable, and the record's statement that MT applies is the error C-032 corrects. The replacement keeps the conclusion and every accepted route, including a statement of the MT exclusion in § 740.20(b)(2)(iii), and adds the exclusions that apply to this module whichever ECCN governs and the § 744.9(e) bar.
