## Defect
Criterion C-028 in tasks/capital-markets/extract-key-terms-from-section-16-filings/task.json (title line 237, text line 241) credits the report only if it treats the Form 5 reporting of Kuznetsov's October 8, 2024 gift as a delinquent filing and fails a report that does not identify late reporting. The transaction is an acquisition of 2,500 shares by bona fide gift from a family member. Under 17 CFR 240.16a-3(g)(1) a Form 4 is required for dispositions by bona fide gift; under 240.16a-3(f)(1) other transactions exempt from section 16(b) are reported on Form 5 within 45 days after the issuer's fiscal year end, and 240.16b-5 exempts both the acquisition and the disposition of securities by bona fide gift. The issuer's fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, so the Form 5 was due February 14, 2025, the day it was filed. A report that applies the rule correctly fails C-028 and, because every criterion must be met, the task.

C-035 (title line 293, text line 297) fixes the deadline for Holloway's Form 4 for the January 8, 2025 transaction at January 10, 2025, calls the January 14, 2025 filing two business days late, and fails a report that does not compute the deadline as January 10. Rule 16a-3(g)(1) requires the Form 4 before the end of the second business day following the day of execution. Executive Order 14133 closed the executive departments and agencies on Thursday, January 9, 2025, and the SEC announced that EDGAR would be closed that day and that the day "will be treated as a federal holiday for filing purposes". The two business days after Wednesday, January 8, 2025 were Friday, January 10 and Monday, January 13, so the deadline was January 13 and the January 14 filing was one business day late. A report that computes the deadline correctly fails C-035.

C-038 (title line 317, text line 321) and C-039 (title line 325, text line 329) treat the February 1, 2025 vesting and settlement of 20,000 of Narayanan's restricted stock units as a purchase matchable with her November 14, 2024 sale of 75,000 shares at $39.85, and C-039 requires $797,000 as the potential disgorgement. The RSUs are derivative securities under 17 CFR 240.16a-1(c): each is a right to one share of common stock at a fixed price of $0.00, and both of Narayanan's Forms 4 report them in Table II. Under 240.16b-6(a) the purchase of the underlying stock is the acquisition of the call equivalent position, which the February 2025 Form 4 dates to the grant on November 1, 2023, more than six months before the sale; under 240.16b-6(b) the closing of the position on conversion and "the acquisition of underlying securities at a fixed exercise price due to the exercise or conversion of a call equivalent position" are exempt. No purchase within six months of the sale can be matched with it, and nothing is recoverable from the pairing. A report that applies Rule 16b-6(b) fails C-039. C-052 (line 433), which correctly credits a report that does not treat the November 14, 2024 exercise-and-sale as creating exposure, explains that "the concern should be about the later RSU vesting pairing", repeating the same error.

C-062 (title line 509, text line 513) credits the report if it "determines that Whitmore's January 2025 Form 4 was timely filed (or at minimum does not flag it as late)" and fails it if the filing "is incorrectly flagged as a late filing". The Form 4 reports sales executed on Friday, January 3, 2025 and was filed on Wednesday, January 8, 2025. Under Rule 16a-3(g)(1) the two business days after January 3 were Monday, January 6 and Tuesday, January 7, so the filing was one business day late; the January 9 closure falls after the deadline. The sales were made under a Rule 10b5-1 plan, and Rule 16a-3(g)(2) deems the date on which the broker notifies the reporting person of the execution to be the date of execution where the plan satisfies the affirmative defense conditions of Rule 10b5-1(c) and the reporting person does not select the date, subject to Rule 16a-3(g)(4), which caps the deemed date at the third business day after the trade date, here January 8. The Form 4 leaves its "Deemed Execution Date" column blank, and no task document gives a notification date. A report that applies Rule 16a-3(g)(1) to the filing as made and flags it as one business day late fails C-062, while a report that calls the filing timely without stating a basis passes.

## Objections considered
- Objection: The reporting person's own Form 5 (footnote F2) admits the transaction should have been on Form 4, and the client memo calls it a delinquency, so the rubric follows the record.
  Answer: The instructions ask for a timeliness assessment of the filings, which is a question of law under Rule 16a-3. Footnote F2 cites "Rule 16a-3(g)" for the duty, and (g)(1) reaches gifts only as dispositions. The rubric elsewhere credits a solver for catching errors in the source filings (C-021, C-026), and a statement in a source document that is wrong in law is the same kind of item.
- Objection: An acquisition by gift is a transaction "not exempt from section 16(b)" and so falls in the first limb of (g)(1).
  Answer: Rule 16b-5 exempts "Both the acquisition and the disposition" by bona fide gift, and, of transactions by gift, (f)(1)(i)(A) carves out of Form 5 only "dispositions by bona fide gifts". An exempt acquisition by gift is therefore a Form 5 item under (f)(1)(i). Footnote F1 records a bona fide gift for no consideration.
- Objection: January 9, 2025 is not one of the legal public holidays in 5 U.S.C. 6103(a), so it remained a business day for a Section 16 deadline.
  Answer: The SEC, which administers the deadline and receives the filing, announced that the day "will be treated as a federal holiday for filing purposes" and accepted no EDGAR filings that day. A day on which a Form 4 could not be filed is not one of the two business days Rule 16a-3(g)(1) allows. The replacement keeps the late-filing finding; it changes the deadline to January 13 and the delay to one business day.
- Objection: The SEC notice treats as timely on January 10 only filings "required to be made on Thursday, January 9, 2025", so a Form 4 for a January 8 transaction stayed due on January 10.
  Answer: That sentence addresses deadlines that fell on January 9; a Form 4 for a January 8 transaction had no deadline on January 9. The notice also treats January 9 "as a federal holiday for filing purposes", so it is not one of the two business days after January 8, which are January 10 and January 13.
- Objection: C-038 asks only for a "potential" pairing and C-040 separately asks for the Rule 16b-3 exemption, so the rubric already leaves room for a conclusion that no liability arises.
  Answer: C-039 requires $797,000 as "the potential Section 16(b) disgorgement" and fails a report whose calculation is "missing", so a report that concludes there is nothing to disgorge fails it, and C-052 tells the judge that the RSU pairing is the concern. Rule 16b-6(b) exempts the settlement whether or not the grant met the approval conditions of Rule 16b-3(d), and the grant itself is more than six months from the sale, so the exposure the criteria describe does not exist on the record. The replacements still require the pairing to be addressed, and C-039 allows the $797,000 figure as the amount that would be at issue.
- Objection: The SEC's ownership form code M, used for the RSU settlement, refers to Rule 16b-3, so the exemption turns on Rule 16b-3 conditions the record does not show.
  Answer: The code describes how the transaction was reported; it does not displace Rule 16b-6(b), whose note states that an exercise or conversion that does not satisfy that rule is eligible for exemption under Rule 16b-3. A $0.00 fixed-price RSU is not an out-of-the-money right, so Rule 16b-6(b) applies without regard to Rule 16b-3.
- Objection: Whitmore's sales were made under a Rule 10b5-1 plan, so Rule 16a-3(g)(2) makes the broker's notification date the date of execution and the January 8 filing was timely.
  Answer: Rule 16a-3(g)(2) applies only where the plan satisfies the affirmative defense conditions of Rule 10b5-1(c) and the reporting person does not select the date of execution, and it makes the notification date, not the trade date plus a fixed period, the date of execution. The Form 4 reports no deemed execution date and the record gives no notification date, so timeliness on that basis is a conclusion the record does not support. The replacement still credits a report that reaches it and states its basis; it no longer fails a report that applies Rule 16a-3(g)(1) to the filing as made.
