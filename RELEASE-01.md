# Package 01

16 corrections for 6 publishers against 8 public repositories: Harvey, LegalQuants, Anthropic, General Legal, Crosby Legal, Thomson Reuters. 13 are rated high severity, 2 medium, 1 low. 15 of the 16 carry a patch. 0 of the 16 have been filed. Each correction is offered through the channel the publisher's repository provides; once it is filed, the entry links to the pull request or issue and records the dates it was filed, first answered and resolved.

The counts say where corrections were found among the files that were read. They are not a measure of any publisher's quality and do not support a comparison between publishers: the artifacts differ in size by orders of magnitude and were not read to the same depth.

Each correction gives what the artifact says, what the governing source says, and the entry, which quotes both with file, line and URL. What counts as a correction, how each was verified, and the limits of the register are in the [README](README.md).

## LegalQuants

### [LegalQuants/lq-plugin-oss](targets/legalquants-lq-plugin-oss/TARGET.md) (3)

- The discovery reference's Rule 45 summary misstates the Rule in seven places. It says the court "may" protect a person from significant expense, where Rule 45(d)(2)(B)(ii) says an order compelling production over an objection "must" protect a person who is neither a party nor a party's officer. The same file misstates Rules 26, 33, 34, 36 and 37 and FRE 501 and 502 in seventeen further paragraphs. The skill's drafting-patterns reference repeats the Rule 33(d), Rule 36(a)(4) and Rule 26(b)(5)(B) misstatements and says that a response to an interrogatory "may answer, object specifically, or state that the investigation is continuing", where Rule 33(b)(3) requires each interrogatory, to the extent it is not objected to, to be answered separately and fully in writing under oath. [LQ-002](targets/legalquants-lq-plugin-oss/findings/LQ-002.md).
- The privilege-log authority row links the Fifth Circuit's May 2017 opinion in EEOC v. BDO USA, which the panel withdrew and replaced on November 16, 2017. Five further rows of the same source ledger misdescribe their sources: Local Civil Rule 33.3, listed for both districts, applies in the Southern District only; the coverage credited to Civil L.R. 37-1 appears in the Commentary to Civil L.R. 37-3; a standing order's conditional expectation is stated as a requirement; the S.D. Fla. Local Rules link is to the superseded December 1, 2023 compilation; and one magistrate judge's earlier discovery procedures are labelled "S.D. Fla. Discovery Handbook". [LQ-003](targets/legalquants-lq-plugin-oss/findings/LQ-003.md).
- A dated "point in time" page of an Act on legislation.gov.uk passes the skill's currency check as the confirmed current text. The same UK registry entry has no marker for the status line "This version of this provision no longer has effect" or for an annotation recording that the whole provision shown has been repealed, revoked or omitted; its prospective marker matches only "This version of this Act contains provisions that are prospective", so the status lines of a single prospective provision and of an instrument or Schedule that contains prospective provisions are not recognised; and its as-enacted marker does not match a statutory instrument's status line "as it was originally made". It tells the model that every effect listed under "Changes and effects yet to be applied" is an amendment already in force and that the list means "the words you just read are wrong", where on the pages checked the publisher's banner states that the text is up to date with all changes known to be in force and the listed effects are amendments not yet in force; it introduces five URL forms as "All four"; and it says Explanatory Notes "are not evidence of what the Act means", where the UK Supreme Court holds that explanatory notes may cast light on the meaning of particular statutory provisions. It also says correction slips "change the text without an amending instrument", where the publisher produces a correction slip only for minor errors that do not change the meaning of the legislation, makes the correction on the website copy but not in the original print PDF, and corrects substantive errors by new legislation. [LQ-004](targets/legalquants-lq-plugin-oss/findings/LQ-004.md).

[CFL-137](targets/anthropics-claude-for-legal/findings/CFL-137.md).

## General Legal

### [General-Legal/legal-templates](targets/general-legal-legal-templates/TARGET.md) (2)

- Section 541.102(b) of the Texas Business and Commerce Code prescribes the notice of sale of sensitive personal data word for word: "NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data." Both privacy policy templates paraphrase it, and neither offers the parallel biometric data notice that subsection (c) prescribes. In their state privacy rights notice both templates also describe the California right to know without "sharing" (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.110(a)(3) and 1798.115(a)(2)) and describe the right to limit use of sensitive personal information (§ 1798.121(a)) as a right to stop processing for inference, and their Shine the Light paragraphs omit the third parties' addresses and the examples of products or services that § 1798.83(a)(2) requires. The European notice of the GDPR template states that the U.S. is not the subject of an adequacy decision, although Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795 covers transfers to organisations in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. It names "The Information Commissioner's Office" as the UK regulator, an office abolished on 30 September 2026 and replaced by the Information Commission, at the Wilmslow address the regulator no longer gives. Both templates also lost the text of every hyperlink in their Word originals (14 in the U.S. template, 21 in the GDPR template), because scripts/convert_docx.py does not read hyperlinks. [GLT-022](targets/general-legal-legal-templates/findings/GLT-022.md).
- The business associate agreement makes the duty to return or destroy PHI at termination depend on a written request from the customer, and nowhere authorizes the customer to terminate if the business associate violates a material term. Four further required terms are missing or narrowed: uses and disclosures are allowed where "Applicable Law" permits them rather than only as required by law ((e)(2)(ii)(A)); there is no covenant to comply with Subpart E when carrying out the customer's obligations ((e)(2)(ii)(H)); access for the Secretary is limited to what the customer needs for its own reporting ((e)(2)(ii)(I)); and subcontractors need accept only "substantially similar" terms and no Security Rule obligation ((e)(2)(ii)(D), § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(B)). The "Security Incident" definition departs from 45 C.F.R. § 164.304, which the reporting clause invokes; the standing notice of "unsuccessful" incidents takes in successful denial of service attacks and unauthorized access that goes no further, which § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) requires to be reported; and the "Secure" definition states that the HHS guidance requires encryption of electronic information, although the guidance also accepts clearing, purging or destruction of the media. The reporting clause limits the identification of affected individuals in a breach notification to device identifiers, where 45 C.F.R. § 164.410(c)(1) requires the identification of each individual to the extent possible. The breach report clause lists four items of content and omits the other available information that § 164.410(c)(2) requires the business associate to provide for the notification of individuals under § 164.404(c). [GLT-008](targets/general-legal-legal-templates/findings/GLT-008.md).
